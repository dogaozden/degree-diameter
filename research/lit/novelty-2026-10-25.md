## Claim 1: multi-level covers of H(q) with hub vertices, bipartite D = 7

**Verdict: partial overlap.**

Prior art for the base graph (no hubs):

- **Gómez–Fiol compounds with a tournament.** J. Gómez, M. A. Fiol, "Dense compound graphs", Ars Combin. 20-A (1985) 211–237 [meta].
  Comellas' 2026 table lists the operation "B(T): Compound using a bipartite graph B and a tournament T [23]" with [23] = this paper
  (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.18994 [read]). This is the general-graph form of "levels Z_{2r+1}, one matching per tournament arc". I could not read the 1985 paper.
- **The wiki's D = 7 column.** CombinatoricsWiki, "The Degree Diameter Problem for Bipartite Graphs",
  http://combinatoricswiki.org/wiki/The_Degree_Diameter_Problem_for_Bipartite_Graphs [read]; same numbers as Table 4 of
  M. Miller, J. Širáň, DS14v2 (2013), https://www.combinatorics.org/files/Surveys/ds14/ds14v2-2013.pdf [read]. For Δ = 6..16 at D = 7 the cell colour is
  "found by Delorme, Gómez, Quisquater". The cited source "On large bipartite graphs" is marked "submitted"; I found no published version.
  Cells (7,7), (9,7)–(16,7) equal (2r+1)·|incidence graph of H(q)| (checked numerically); (6,7) = 8,310 and (8,7) = 40,586 are Delorme's §9 construction below.
- **Delorme 1985, §9 "Insertion de sommets sur les arêtes"** (https://web.mat.upc.edu/francesc.comellas/delta-d/papers/81981341.pdf
  [read]). Inserting 2q³ vertices on edges of the hexagon incidence graph gives a bipartite, 2-connected graph of degree q+1 and
  diameter 7 on 2q(q⁴+q³+3q²+q+1) vertices. I checked this formula reproduces the table's (6,7) = 8,310 (q = 5) and (8,7) = 40,586 (q = 7).
  This is a D = 7 hexagon-plus-new-vertices construction, but vertices are inserted on edges rather than attached as hubs.
- **Delorme 1985, §3–4.** Multi-level products: vertex set Z/mZ × Q^k with a duality f of a generalized quadrangle or hexagon
  (§3), and twisted products G*F₄, G*C₁₉, G*C₃₅, G*S₃ (§4) [read]. These are cyclic multi-level graphs over generalized polygons, using a *global* duality or group voltages, not a bijection chosen at will.

Overlap on hubs:

- **Rajiv 2026** (above, §4, Theorem 3) attaches "t new vertices, each joined to one point and one line of every block" of a
  tournament compound of m copies of a polygon incidence graph, joins the new vertices by a graph Y of diameter ≤ n, and shows the diameter stays n
  [read]. That is the hub construction, for general graphs of diameter n = 4, 6. The lab's graphs are bipartite with diameter n+1 = 7, and hubs
  are split into line-side and point-side classes.
- **Comellas, 2 Oct 2026 entry for (Δ,D) = (4,6)** on https://web.mat.upc.edu/francesc.comellas/delta-d/desc_g/desc_g6.html [read]:
  "From the generalized hexagon H₃ … incidence edges … are deleted to free degree, and the resulting capacity is used to attach new vertices, each to one point and one line, while an internal 2-factor supplies their remaining degree."
- **Bhakar, Yugeswardeenoo, 7–15 Oct 2026 entries** on the same page [read]: subdivide hexagon edges (often along a Hermitian spread), join the new vertices by an external graph. D = 6, general graphs.
- **Older vertex-replacement and compounding in hexagons** (all D = 6, general graphs): J. Gómez, I. Pelayo, C. Balbuena, Networks 34 (1999)
  154–161 [abstract; the UPC copy I tried returned an HTML page]; G. Pineda-Villavicencio, J. Gómez, M. Miller, H. Pérez-Rosés, Networks 53 (2009) 315–328 [abstract]
  (https://dro.deakin.edu.au/articles/journal_contribution/New_largest_known_graphs_of_diameter_6/20751205); F. Comellas, J. Gómez, arXiv:math/9411218 [read, intro].

Nothing found: any source giving a bipartite graph of diameter 7 built from (2r+1) copies of H(q) *plus* hub vertices.

## Claim 2: surgery, typed levels, mirrored surgery, relaxed sets

### 2a. One-transition surgery with freed non-collinear sets; typed levels. **Verdict: partial overlap (close).**

- **Rajiv 2026** [read]: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23052995 (PDF in the record). Section 4, Definition 3: compound C(I; T, H) of m
  copies of the incidence graph I of a generalized n-gon, with a *type-changing* matching x_i ~ x̄_j (point to line, for a bijection x ↦ x̄) when i → j in a tournament T, and a *type-preserving*
  matching x_i ~ x_j or ℓ_i ~ ℓ_j otherwise. Lemma 5: deleting from a type-preserving matching the edges at a set of pairwise non-collinear points (or pairwise non-concurrent lines)
  keeps the diameter at most n; the proof uses only a shortest path, and "applies to hexagons as well, where a set of pairwise non-collinear points can be much larger than an ovoid". Theorem 3 adds hubs (claim 1).
  This is the lab's "friendly transition" (identity matching, types H ↔ H^D) with (H1)–(H3) reduced to (H1). Differences I can see: the compounds there are general graphs of diameter n (6), the lab's typed graphs are bipartite with diameter 7;
  m = 4, 5 only; the paper's hubs have one point-slot and one line-slot per block, the lab's are one-sided. The paper's results: (13,6), (15,6), (16,6) with H(9), H(11). Its table lists freed sets of 3,875–7,369 elements for q = 9, 11.
- **Related by the same author**, Zenodo 2026-09-30, https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23056128 [abstract]: "For every bijection σ from the points to the lines of a generalized hexagon, the graph on the points in which x and y are adjacent whenever one of them lies on the image of the other has diameter at most 5" (a D = 5 quotient statement). Same "any bijection σ" principle as the lab's base graph, at D = 5.
- **The freed sets are known objects.** A set of points pairwise at point-graph distance ≥ 2 (non-collinear) is a *partial distance-2 ovoid* of H(q); the dual hexagon H(q)^D gives non-concurrent lines; pairwise opposite points are a partial ovoid (partial distance-3 ovoid).
  Definitions: A. Bishnoi, F. Ihringer, arXiv:1606.07288 [read, Def. 2.4, 2.5]; A. Offer, H. Van Maldeghem, Discrete Math. 294 (2005) 147–160 [meta]. Distance-2 ovoids of H(q) are known only for q = 2, 3, 4
  (A. De Wispelaere, H. Van Maldeghem, Discrete Math. 308 (2008) 2976–2983 [meta], cited in Bishnoi–Ihringer [read]), and H(q) ≅ H(q)^D only for q a power of 3
  ([read] in Bishnoi–Ihringer, citing Van Maldeghem 1998). I found no source with large explicit partial distance-2 ovoids of H(11) or H(13) (the lab has 8,645 points / 8,246 lines at q = 11).
  Hoffman-type bound q⁴+q²+1 is quoted by the lab; I did not find where it is published for H(q).
- **Delorme 1985 §8(b)** [read]: for the split Cayley hexagon, a set of q³+1 vertices at mutual distance 6 (citing Thas 1980) is duplicated to raise degree by one at D = 6. The Hermitian spread of H(q) is used in the lab for the opposite sets; Delorme's duplication of such sets is the earliest use I found of pairwise-opposite sets in degree-diameter constructions.
- **Typed levels as such.** Alternating H and H^D levels is, in the incidence graph, a relabelling of which class is "points". Delorme 1985 §3 uses a duality of a polygon to build the multi-level graph [read]; the Rajiv compound has the same type-preserving/type-changing structure. I found no source that makes the H/H^D assignment into a bipartite 2-colouring giving diameter n+1.

### 2b. Mirrored surgery with local duality σ(Γ₂(x)) = Γ₂(σ(x)). **Verdict: nothing found in these sources.**

- Rajiv 2026 cuts only type-preserving matchings [read]; the type-changing matchings are never cut. No (H3)-type condition appears.
- Global dualities: Delorme 1985 §1–3 [read]. Polarities and dualities exist for H(q) only when q is a power of 3 (Bishnoi–Ihringer, above). The local condition on Γ₂(x) for a *non-self-dual* H(q), q = 11, 13, is not in any source I read.
- Searches for "local duality" with generalized hexagon/polygon returned nothing defining the term (WebSearch, standard mode).
- Related only: Rajiv's dualities of order four of H(9) (https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22907863 [abstract]) and Delorme's polarity quotients; both use global dualities of H(3^h).

### 2c. Relaxed freed sets with collinear pairs. **Verdict: nothing found in these sources.**

Rajiv's Lemma 5 requires *pairwise* non-collinear (resp. non-concurrent) sets [read]. I found no source that allows collinear pairs under hub-slot conditions.

## Claim 4: AS(q) weighted 2-ovoid (Theorem A); GQ copy theorem (Theorem B)

### 4a. Theorem A. **Verdict: nothing found in these sources.**

Results of a helper agent's search [agent], re-checked where marked. No source found that gives an ovoid, m-ovoid, weighted ovoid or 2-cover of AS(q) = P(W(q),x) for q odd, nor one built from two elliptic quadrics through the centre x. Zenodo search for "Ahrens-Szekeres": 0 records.

Related (not overlapping the statement):

- J. Bamberg, A. Devillers, J. Schillewaert, "Weighted intriguing sets of finite generalised quadrangles", J. Algebraic Combin. 36 (2012) 149–173,
  https://www.maths.tcd.ie/EMIS/journals/JACO/Volume36_1/3705773715058068.fulltext.pdf [read, rechecked, §7]. Weighted m-ovoids exist as a notion. Lemma 7.1: if x is a regular point of a GQ of order (s,s) and O an ovoid containing x, then O∖{x} is an ovoid of the Payne derivation G^x.
  Lemma 7.2 partitions G^x into ovoids under hypotheses that exclude W(3,q), q odd (it has no ovoids). So the odd-q derived quadrangle is exactly where ovoid methods stop, and no weighted example is given.
- J. Bamberg, M. Law, T. Penttila, "Tight sets and m-ovoids of generalised quadrangles", Combinatorica 29 (2009) 1–17 [abstract]: m-ovoids of W(3,q), q odd, every even m. In W(q), not in AS(q).
- A. Cossidente, C. Culbert, G. L. Ebert, G. Marino, "On m-ovoids of W₃(q)", Finite Fields Appl. 14 (2008) 76–84 [meta]; G. L. Ebert, "Partitioning projective geometries into caps", Canad. J. Math. 37 (1985) 1163–1175 [meta]: 2-ovoids of W(3,q) built from classes of an elliptic-quadric partition. The lab's note says it could not read these; neither could I.
- T. Feng, W. Li, R. Tao, arXiv:2208.13023, Remark 3.21 [agent]: transitive 2-ovoids of W(3,q) known; nothing on AS(q). T. Feng, Y. Wang, Q. Xiang, arXiv:1909.07954 [abstract].
- T. Feng, W. Li, "The point regular automorphism groups of the Payne derived quadrangle of W(q)", J. Combin. Theory Ser. A 179 (2021) 105384, arXiv:1908.10659 [abstract]: same GQ, no ovoid material.
- R. McCulloch, arXiv:2408.00887 [read, §3]: reports computer-found ovoids in the dual of P(W(q),x) for the first few q (the helper's "always possesses ovoids" is an overstatement; the text says "for the first few values of q"). Dually these are spreads of AS(q).
- Standard references I could not open: Payne–Thas, *Finite Generalized Quadrangles* (2nd ed., EMS 2009); Thas–Payne, "Spreads and ovoids in finite generalized quadrangles", Geom. ### 4b. Theorem B (copy theorem with stand-ins). **Verdict: partial overlap.**

- **Delorme 1985 (Eur. J. Combin. 6, 291–302)** [read, full text]: §7 "Augmentation du degré": for a graph G and an induced subgraph A, "dédoubler A" adds a copy of A in which an outside vertex x is adjacent to the copy y exactly when x is adjacent to the original of y. With nested sets A₀ ⊃ A₁ ⊃ …, degree +1, diameter D, bipartite preserved ("je ne donne pas la preuve"); degree +r by "(r+1)-upling". §8(a): for the GQ W(3,q)-type quadrangle, A₁ = a vertex at mutual distance 4 with the q²+1 spread vertices; degree q+2 and q²+q+4 more vertices; q = 5 gives 312+34 = 346 vertices (the old table value).
  The lab's NOTE already credits this. In the general statement of §7, A is an arbitrary induced subgraph (so it may contain vertices of both sides); the examples in §8 copy vertices of one side only.
- **Delorme, JGT 9 (1985) 325–334**, https://doi.org/10.1002/jgt.3190090304 [abstract from Crossref]: bipartite constructions with lim inf B·Δ^{1−D} > 0 for every D ≥ 2 (equal to 1 for D = 2, 3, 4, 6 per the survey). Full text not accessible, so the concrete diameter-4 bound there is unchecked.
- **Araujo-Pardo, Dalfó, Fiol, López, arXiv:2103.11443** [agent]: Theorem 4.3 "semi-double" (twin duplication of every vertex of one side, same neighbourhood) and Theorem 4.5(ii) for a GQ. Full-side twinning, not weighted copies.
- **Araujo-Pardo, López, arXiv:2005.02427** [read]: diameter 3 only; Problem 4 asks for diameters 4, 6.
- **Delorme, Farhi, IEEE Trans. Comput. C-33 (1984) 857–860** [meta]: not accessible; the lab's "[DF84]" attribution of the copy lemma is unchecked by me. Delorme 1985 §7 is what I read.
- **Old table values**: DS14v2 Table 4 (Δ = 7, 11, 13, 15, 16 at D = 4: 346, 1,734, 3,064, 4,946, 5,134) [read]; no larger post-2013 value found (the wiki's current text was fetched once, with the same numbers).

Nothing found: weights greater than one on points, simultaneous line copies attached to stand-ins, the statement n_b(Δ,4) ≥ 2q²Δ for q ≤ Δ−1, or an AS(q) construction.

## Claim 5: Haar graphs of SL(2,q)/PSL(2,q), and products with cyclic groups, bipartite D = 8–10

**Verdict: partial overlap.** The construction is not claimed in the sources for *bipartite degree-diameter*, but the same groups and the same dipole-lift form are used for girth and for general-graph degree-diameter.

Results of a helper agent's search [agent]; items marked "[read, rechecked]" I confirmed myself.

- **Exoo–Jajcay, "On the girth of voltage graph lifts", European J. Combin. 32 (2011) 554–562** [meta]. Content via the Dynamic Cage Survey
  (https://www.combinatorics.org/files/Surveys/ds16/ds16v3-2013.pdf [read, rechecked]): the smallest known cubic graph of girth 30 (1,143,408 = 83³ − 83 vertices) is "a voltage graph lift of the θ-graph of order 2 using the group SL(2, 83)". A lift of the θ-graph (two vertices, three parallel edges) is a Haar graph: the same construction as the lab's, used for girth.
- **Bray, Parker, Rowley, "Cayley type graphs and cubic graphs of large girth", Discrete Math. 214 (2000) 113–121** [meta]; DS16 [read, rechecked] lists the cubic graph of girth 27 and order 285,852 = B(PSL(2,83) × Z₃, {α, δ, δ⁻¹}). PSL(2,q) × cyclic, for girth.
- **Estélyi, Pisanski, "Which Haar graphs are Cayley graphs?", Electron. J. Combin. 23(3) (2016) P3.10**, arXiv:1505.01475 [abstract]: Haar graph terminology; no diameter results.
- **LPS and quotients:** Lubotzky–Phillips–Sarnak, Combinatorica 8 (1988) 261–277 [meta]: bipartite Cayley graphs of PGL(2,q); diameter about (4/3) log_p n, so far below the table; N. T. Sardari, Combinatorica 39 (2019) 427–446, arXiv:1511.09340 [agent read]: bipartite LPS diameter > (4/3) log_p n + O(1); numerics for random Cayley graphs of PSL₂(Z/q), non-bipartite, degree 6, orders below the table values.
- **2013 table sources:** the wiki colour key credits the D = 8, 9, 10 cells for Δ ≤ 9 or so to "Eyal Loz as part of the joint project *The degree/diameter problem for several classes of graphs* by Loz, Pérez-Rosés, Pineda-Villavicencio" and (D = 8, Δ ≥ 10, and (3,10)) to Delorme–Gómez–Quisquater [read, rechecked from the page HTML]. DS14v2 §3.4.5: they "adapted the voltage-assignment-based method developed by Loz and Širáň … to the case of bipartite graphs; this resulted in updating more than one third of the table" [read]. Loz–Širáň use semidirect products of cyclic groups (Australas. J. Combin. 41 (2008) 63–80 [agent read]). No PSL(2,q) found in any of these.
- **General-graph 2026 entries using SL(2,q)-type groups** (Comellas table, https://web.mat.upc.edu/francesc.comellas/delta-d/desc_g/desc_g7.html and desc_g6.html [read, rechecked]): (4,7) = 1,344, a regular voltage lift of a two-vertex base graph over SL(2,7):C₂ (Yugeswardeenoo, 19 Oct 2026); hexagon graphs whose external edges are invariant under SL(2,q) acting regularly on spread lines (D = 6). Not bipartite, not Haar graphs of SL(2,q)/PSL(2,q), but the same idea of lifting over these groups.
- **Erskine, Tuite, "Large Cayley graphs of small diameter", arXiv:1708.02425** [agent read]: "to employ matrix groups over finite fields in the degree-diameter problem", SL(3,p) subgroup, diameters 3–5.

Nothing found: a paper or table entry using Haar graphs/bi-Cayley graphs of PSL(2,q), SL(2,q) or PGL(2,q) (or products with cyclic groups) for large bipartite graphs of diameter 8, 9 or 10. Queries on arXiv and Zenodo for "Haar graph" + "degree diameter" and "bi-Cayley" + "diameter" returned nothing relevant.
