## Idea 1: bipartite coset graphs X(G,H,HgH) with a vertex stabilizer (started 2026-10-31)

Graph: G = PGL(2,q), H a cyclic subgroup of order h, g in G with |HgH| = d*h (so H ∩ gHg^-1 has order h/d).
Vertices: left cosets Hx (side A) and left cosets Hy (side B), both N = |G|/h per side.
Edges: Hx ~ Hy iff x y^-1 in HgH. Well defined; degree = |HgH|/h = d. G acts by right multiplication
on both sides, so the graph is transitive on each side. Diameter check = 2 BFS (from one A vertex and one B vertex).

Why it could work (the odds): with d = 10 and h = 10 (target cell: 2N > 1,176,480, so N >= 588,241, |G| = 10N):
- Plain Haar graphs (h = 1): uncovered B vertices from a root ~ Poisson with mean mu = N e^(-W/N), W = 5,380,840
  odd walks of length <= 7. At N = 588K, mu ~ 63 (matches the cell notes).
- Here the stabilizer Stab(u) = x^-1 H x (order h) acts on B and preserves distances from u, so the uncovered
  set from u is a union of Stab(u)-orbits (size h). Independent events: N/h orbits, each uncovered with prob
  e^(-lambda), lambda = W/N = 9.14. Expected uncovered orbits mu_orb = (N/h) e^(-lambda) = 6.3 (h = 10), so
  P(diameter 8) ~ exp(-6.3) ~ 2e-3 per graph, if the orbit model holds. Orbit-symmetric Haar (alpha of order 3,
  the k = 3 family) gets mu/3 and measured tails 2x worse than the model; the test below checks that for coset graphs.
- The family is small: about |G|/(d h) double cosets per group (~59K for PGL(2,181), N = 592,956), and each
  candidate needs one BFS (~1-2 s in C). So a sample of a few hundred is affordable, the whole family is not.

Test plan: (1) small validation at d = 6, h = 6, T = 6 (PGL(2,17), PGL(2,19)) where the orbit model and the
vertex (Haar-like) model give clearly different P(no uncovered vertex); (2) sample the real case
PGL(2,181), d = h = 10, T = 8, and look at the lower tail of uncovered counts.

   zero-uncovered graph. Dihedral H does not exist at q = 17 (nonsplit torus), so no dihedral run there.

2. Real size, PGL(2,181), d = h = 10, T = 8 (N = 592,956, lambda = 9.07), H = C10 (split torus, coset.c):
   every candidate DISCONNECTED (uncA = 296,478 = N/2 exactly). Reason: all elements of C10 have square determinant,
   so every walk keeps the determinant class and the bipartite graph splits into two components. Dead as set up.

3. Real size, PGL(2,181), d = h = 10, T = 8, H = D5 (order 10, generated by an order-5 torus element and the
   non-square involution [[0,1],[nu,0]]; coset.c with HTYPE=D). Connected. The first 40 random regular double cosets
   (coset.c, s181_D_40.tsv): min uncovered B = 150, median about 300, no zero. The Poisson-orbit model predicts
   mean 6.8 orbits = 68 vertices; the measured mean is about 4x that. So the orbit reduction by the stabilizer
   (h = 10) is cancelled by a tax of about 4x from the H-bi-invariance of D (words collide systematically).
   Larger sample: s181_D_1500.tsv (see the summary below).

4. The family itself is small (59,292 regular double cosets for PGL(2,181)), and one BFS costs about 0.7 s.
