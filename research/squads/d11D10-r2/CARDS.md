## Squad A (search and experiments)

  AFROB = 1, 2, 3, 4 (four inequivalent automorphisms), plus AFROB=1 with AR=6 and AR=7 (should be equivalent to other
  AFROB values; check). For each: degenerate fraction (cost > 10⁶), and mean, median, minimum and histogram of cost/10 over
  the non-degenerate samples. Rank the pairings. Budget 7 slot-min.
- **A2: what are the degenerate k = 10 samples?** 20–27 % of k = 10 samples have cost ≈ 56M (71 % of N), the rest ≈ 100.
  Copy haar_sym.c, make RANDORB print the orbit generator s (desc_elem) with each sample's cost, run 60 samples
  (AFROB=1 AFROBH="0 1 2 0" AR=2), and find the property of s (or of its Q-part x) that predicts degeneracy: order of
  x, trace of x, whether x and α(x) commute, the subgroup generated by S's Q-parts, the z-coordinate, … Give a cheap test
  and, if you can, patch your copy so random_orbit() skips such s; show the degenerate fraction drops to 0 and the
  time per sample. Budget 5 slot-min.
- **A3: k = 5 Frobenius anneal, AFROB=2.** ORBIT="5 2 1" AFROB=2 ORBW=5 CKPT=A3/a3.ckpt on psl 243 × Z11,
  SECONDS = 600, K = 8. Report the cost trace (cost every ~2 min), lowest cost, how many uncovered elements are
  α-fixed at the end. Budget 12 slot-min.
- **A4: k = 5 Frobenius anneal, AFROB=4, different schedule.** ORBIT="5 2 1" AFROB=4 ORBW=8 RESTART=1 TARGP=50 COOL=40

  ORBIT="10 1 1" AR=2, run RANDORB=150 for AFROB = 1, 2, 3, 4 with AFROBD=1. Compare with A1's numbers (same columns).
  If one beats every AFROBH pairing, run `RANDORB=1500 RANDB=1 RANDSTOP=1` on it with OUTFILE in your folder for the rest

## Squad B (tools and analysis)

- **B1: prefilter for k = 10 random search.** In a k = 10 sample, α-fixed elements are missed with probability
  e^(−λ/10) ≈ 0.25 each and elements with orbit size 2 with e^(−λ/5) ≈ 0.06, so about 60 % of samples fail on these few
  "small-orbit" elements. Copy haar_sym.c and add `PREF=1` to RANDORB: before the full evaluation, test only the
  small-orbit targets t (one per orbit: α-fixed elements, then orbit sizes 2 and 5; precompute them once by scanning
  z = 0 only, since z ↦ 2z fixes only z = 0) by meet-in-the-middle: t is within distance 9 of (e,A) on the B side iff
  u·t ∈ X5 for some u ∈ U4, where X5 = {s_a s_b⁻¹ s_c s_d⁻¹ s_e} (a bitset over G, 11⁵ words) and
  U4 = {s_a s_b⁻¹ s_c s_d⁻¹} (11⁴ words; e ∈ S so shorter words are included). Skip the full evaluation when a target
  is uncovered (count the sample as cost ≥ 1). Validate: on the same seed, every sample that passes the prefilter gets
  the same cost as without PREF, and every rejected one has full cost ≥ 1 (check 100 samples). Measure seconds per
  sample with and without PREF (RANDB=1 in both). Budget 8 slot-min.
- **B2: speed of one k = 10 sample.** A sample replaces 10 generators (10 table builds) and runs a 9-level BFS:
  `BENCH=10` (no ORBIT) on psl 243 × Z11 gave 308 ms per evaluation, tables 25 ms, levels 6–8 ≈ 70 ms each, level 9
  43 ms. Measure where a k = 10 RANDORB sample spends its 0.37 s (PROFILE=1, or timing code in your copy), try FAST=1/2,
  HWMIN/HWMAX, PFD, HMAX, and report the fastest setting with an identical cost sequence (same seed, RANDORB=30).
  Budget 4 slot-min.
- **B3: why does k = 10 lose more than k = 5?** Random S would give μ/k + small-orbit misses ≈ 6.1 + 1.0 clumps for
  k = 10, but the measured mean is about 9.5 clumps (k = 5 Frobenius: about 12–14 measured against 12.1 + 0.7). In the
  orbit, s_(i+5) = (h x_i h⁻¹, −z_i): the Q-parts come in h-conjugate pairs. Find word identities this forces (pairs of
  different words of length ≤ 9 that are equal for every x), count them per length, and estimate the lost fraction of
  words; compare with ball sizes from `RANDORB=3 LEVELS=3` for k = 10, k = 5 and plain random S (no ORBIT, use
  `RANDSAMPLE` if it exists, else ORBIT="5 2 1" vs "10 1 1"). Suggest an α of order 10 without this loss if there is
  one. Budget 4 slot-min (mostly Python or small C on small groups).
- **B4: checker readiness for psl 243.** Make 2 small VALID solutions over psl 243 with haar_sym (for example
  `ORBIT="5 3 1" AFROB=1 … haar_sym 16 8 120 1 8 OUT psl 243 1`, and one without ORBIT) plus 2 corrupted copies (one
  generator changed, one repeated); run both tools/haarcheck_pq and tools/pqcheck on all 4 and confirm they agree with
  haar_sym's own check. Then time both checkers on a 157.8M-vertex file (any 11 generators over psl 243 × Z11 with
  D=10 header; it will be INVALID, we only want the run time and memory). Budget 6 slot-min.
- **B5: ball-growth census.** On psl 243 × Z11: `RANDORB=4 LEVELS=4` for ORBIT="10 1 1" (AFROB=1 AFROBH="0 1 2 0"
  AR=2), ORBIT="10 1 1" with AFROBD=1, and ORBIT="5 2 1" AFROB=3; record distinct elements per BFS level against
  11·10^(L−1) and find the level where k = 10 falls behind. Write B5/census.md. Budget 3 slot-min.
- **B6: does the cost depend on the class of the orbit generator?** For k = 10, S = {e} ∪ orbit(s) and every orbit has
  exactly one element with z = 1, so s = (x, 1) with x ∈ PSL(2,243). Copy haar_sym.c, make RANDORB print x's trace (as
  a field element), its order and the cost for each sample, run 150 samples (AFROB=1 AFROBH="0 1 2 0" AR=2) and look for
  classes of x with a lower mean cost (e.g. trace in GF(3), elements of order 3, 61, 121, 122, 244, …). If a class is
  clearly better, patch random_orbit() in your copy to sample only that class and measure again. Budget 6 slot-min.
- **B7: wildcard (tools).** Any tool or analysis that would make the 157.8M target (or anything above 148.7M) more likely
  to fall within a few core-hours, not covered by the other cards. Budget 5 slot-min.
- **B8: wildcard (constructions).** A construction route to (11,10) above 148.7M that is not a haar_sym search: lifts,
