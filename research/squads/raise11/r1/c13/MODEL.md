# c13: clump model for odd-D (D = 11) Haar graphs, and the best (p, n) per cell for Δ = 7..14

Files: model.py (model, `pick` ranking), run_pick.py (writes picks.txt), picks.txt (full ranking output),
rr_sl53_37.log (1500 RANDORB samples), val7a.txt / val7b.txt (RANDORB medians for 6 more groups).

## Model
Per side, the expected number of independent clumps of uncovered elements (holes come in orbits of the group
generated by alpha and inversion; the probability of a clump being uncovered is exp(-lambda/size)):
- generic (mu/6): alpha x inversion orbits of size 6, mu = N exp(-lambda), lambda = W/N, W = 1 + sum_i d(d-1)^(2i-1)
- involutions (I e^(-lambda/2)/3): I = 1 (SL, -I) or p(p+eps)/2 (PSL), eps = +1 if p = 1 mod 4 else -1; n odd so z = 0
- alpha-fixed (F e^(-lambda/3)/2): F = p -+ 1 (SL, centralizer of the order-3 element), half of that for PSL; pairs x, x^-1
Central (shared by both sides, Lemma 3 of ODD_D_HOLES.md): (number of alpha-orbits on central elements, identity
excluded) * exp(-lambda/5), h = 5.
K_clump = 2 * (generic + involution + fixed) + central.
Empirical large-n penalty: K_pen = kappa n^2 / 6, kappa = 0.007 (SL), 0.004 (PSL); cost-to-clump factor 6.
K = K_clump + K_pen; P(cost 0) ~ exp(-K).

The penalty is NOT derived. It was fitted (roughly, by hand) on the 4 groups with n >= 100 in the table below, so it
is in-sample for those. The n-dependence is the largest effect in the data: holes grow like n^2 for large n, and the
central-element term alone underpredicts it by 10-30x at n = 811-1129.

## Caveats
- K_clump for the involution and fixed terms uses clump sizes 3 and 2 from the lemma; the involution counts for PSL are
  large (e.g. I = 24753 at p = 223), so PSL n = 1 groups are dominated by the involution term. The measured medians
  for PSL n = 1 are consistent with this (164 vs 132), but the sample is one group.
- Small-n SL with n = 7, 13, 19 at Δ >= 12: kappa penalty is near zero, so the ranking depends mostly on the
  generic and fixed terms; these are the main predictions and they are untested.
