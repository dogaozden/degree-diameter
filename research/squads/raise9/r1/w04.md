# w04: WILDCARD (non-Haar): voltage lifts of small base graphs for (3,9), (4,9) or (5,9)
Values: (3,9) 506 and (4,9) 4,970 (2013 table, never beaten), (5,9) 29,756 (Haar). A Haar graph is a lift of the
dipole (2 vertices, Δ parallel edges). Lifts of a bigger base graph B (b vertices, bipartite, Δ-regular multigraph) over
a group G give b|G| vertices and have more freedom (one voltage per edge, not just per generator). Earlier sessions
(research/lifts/RESULTS.md, research/squads/LOG.md) ran "rich and wide" lift sweeps at orders 4,972-4,988 for (4,9)
and 508-540 for (3,9) (best cost 18), probably with cyclic/metacyclic voltage groups.
New angles to choose from: (a) voltage groups that are odd-order Frobenius groups Z_p x| Z_k (k = 9..63) - the D = 11
lead (no involutions, no centre), for (4,9) with base K_{2,2} with doubled edges (4 vertices, |G| >= 1,243) or the
3+3 base K_{3,3} plus a perfect matching for... (degree 4); (b) base graphs with a non-trivial automorphism and
voltages invariant under it (orbit idea for lifts); (c) bases with b = 6-14 vertices and cyclic groups Z_m (fast
exhaustive-ish annealing). Write a small C or Python annealer: state = voltages on a spanning-tree-reduced edge set,
cost = number of (base vertex pair, group element) combinations at distance > 9 from a vertex in each base orbit.
Build the best lift as an edge list and check it: `$B/apsp EDGES d 9`. Folder r1/w04/.
