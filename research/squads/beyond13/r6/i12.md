# i12 — theory: why random S beats orbit S at small Δ (a first-moment count)
At Δ = 4 the alpha-orbit form S = {e, x, ax, a^2x} has only ~N/3 candidates; random S has ~N^3/6. If a candidate succeeds
with probability P (about e^(-mu') with mu' the expected number of holes, larger for structured S), the expected number of
solutions is (#candidates) * P. Make this quantitative with data already in the squad folders: RANDORB statistics of
orbit S (r1/c14, r4/g07, r5/h12, r5/h13) and random-S first-evaluation costs (r2/e14, r2/e11). Estimate, per Δ and N, the
mu' at which each form still has >= 1 expected solution, and compare with where each form stopped (tables in
research/frontier/beyond/BEYOND_TABLE.md and research/squads/beyond13/r6/HEADER.md). Write research/squads/beyond13/r6/i12/
CANDIDATES.md (short, numbers). Score 0; the rule in "next".
