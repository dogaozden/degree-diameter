## Method

research/lowmem/README.md: the orbit search of research/structured (S closed under α = conjugation by an element of
order 3 times z ↦ rz), with a new evaluator that needs two N-bit bitsets instead of ~14 bytes per element.

Why this cell needed new code, not a new idea: the size law (research/frontier/SIZE_LAW.md §3.3) predicts that
random-S annealing reaches only ~2.33·10⁹ vertices here (+1 %, "marginal; N ≈ 1.2 G, out of reach on this box"),
and the orbit method, which reached +9 % at (14,10), could not be run: haar_sym needs ~11–14 bytes per element,
~14–17 GB at N = 1.2·10⁹. With rows = cosets of U × Z_n the whole state is two bitsets (0.29–0.31 GB per run) and one
evaluation takes 0.9 s on one core, so four runs fit on this 4-core, 16 GB box at once.

The difficulty of an order is μ = N e^(−W/N) with W = 15(1 + 14² + 14⁴ + 14⁶ + 14⁸) = 22,250,358,075 binding words
(D = 10: words of odd length ≤ 9 from (e,A) must reach all of G on side B). The table value corresponds to μ = 4.5:
even random S nearly works there. Each +1 % in N multiplies μ by about e^(0.19) ≈ 1.2 (d ln μ / d ln N = 1 + W/N ≈ 19).

## What was tried and what did not help

- **Squad round d15D10-r1** (8 Haiku cards; research/squads/LOG.md): search parameters K, TARGP, ORBW, the choice of α,
  fixed generators (f = 3, 6), orbit size k = 5 and screening of ~25 groups by the cost of random symmetric S at full
  scale. Nothing beat the defaults (K 8, TARGP 70, ORBW 1, f 0, k 3). The useful findings were about groups: cyclic
  factors n > 1000 give heavy-tailed costs (mean 100–540 at μ ≈ 20), and big p with tiny n (p ≥ 300) evaluates
  1.5–7.5× slower because the rows get short (p² rows of pn bits). Good shapes are p ≈ 130–200 with n ≈ 100–800.
- **Other evaluators considered and dropped.** Storing one row per α-orbit (rows = cosets of the torus C(g) × Z_n,
  so that α acts by sub-row permutations) needs short sub-rows of p ± 1 bits; by my estimate it would be ~1.5× faster at
  best, worth about +0.2 % in N. A sparse "row-local push" for levels 6–7 would save ~30 % per evaluation. Neither is
  worth the risk with a few hours left: the gain per 10× compute is only ~+2 % in N (evaluations to solve grow like
  e^(μ/4) and d ln μ / d ln N ≈ 19).
