## Second session: why orbits of size 5 win here

The orbit model (research/structured/STRUCTURE.md §1): with S invariant under α of order k, P(cost 0) ≈ e^(−μ_eff),
μ_eff = μ/k + F, F = |Fix(α)| e^(−λ/k), λ = W/N. STRUCTURE.md found k = 3 best at D = 7–8, where λ ≈ 7–10 makes a
fixed point expensive for k ≥ 5 (e^(−λ/5) = 0.14–0.25). At (15,10) λ ≈ 17.6, so a fixed point costs only
e^(−17.6/5) = 0.03, and PSL(2,p) halves the torus (|Fix| = (p ∓ 1)/2): F ≈ 1.5–3 for p ≈ 100–200, against μ/3 − μ/5 =
2μ/15 ≈ 4.7 at μ = 35. Round 1 had found k = 5 "a tie" on SL(2,191) × Z181, where |Fix| = 190 gives F = 5.6.
Measured (RANDORB "units" = uncovered α-orbits + uncovered fixed points; research/squads/d15D10-r2):

| μ | k = 3 (SL, best groups) | k = 5 (PSL, p ≈ 130–200, small prime n) |
|---|---|---|
| ≈ 26 | — | median 8–9 units (incl. F ≈ 1.5–2.2) |
| ≈ 27.5 | median ≈ 11 units | — |
| ≈ 35 | — | median 10 (incl. F = 2.8); psl 181 × Z431 tracks μ/5 + F |
| 41.9 | — | median 9.0, mean 9.4, var 11 after FIXPRE (150 samples, psl 139 × Z961) |

On the (15,8) proxy psl 31 × Z601 (μ 27.45) k = 5 annealing solved 3/3 (729–3,745 evaluations) and k = 3 0/3
(best 6–7 after 33k). k = 15 (one orbit) is dead: p must be 29 or 31, so n ~ 10⁵ and the cyclic factor collapses
(median cost 92,808 at μ 29). Small p with big n is bad in general (the z-parts of 15 generators in a huge Z_n leave
large Fourier coefficients; c8 measured Spearman 0.45 between cost and A(S) = max_ξ |(1/15) Σ e(ξ z_i/n)|), and
composite n looked ~2 units worse than prime n (c3).

**FIXPRE** (haar_lean.c, fixcheck/fixcheck2): before the full 1.3 s evaluation, an exact meet-in-the-middle test
(even ball of depth 4 from (e,A), odd ball of depth 5 from (e,B), matched by the coset invariant (y g y⁻¹, z) of the
torus T = C(g)) tells in ~0.05 s whether all α-fixed points are covered; candidates that miss one are redrawn. That
removes F from the exponent at the price of e^F cheap tests. Validated against the full evaluator on ~150 samples
(SL and PSL, split and non-split tori, D = 8 and 10): identical counts.

With m = 3 orbits each annealing move redraws a third of S, which changes ~97 % of the length-9 words, so the search
is close to random sampling: evaluations to solve ≈ 1/P(cost 0) of a prefiltered random S. 217 evaluations solved
μ = 26.4 and μ = 35 (luckier than e^(units) would predict).

**The μ ≈ 42 wall (what the later hours showed).** Rungs at μ = 41.9 (PSL(2,139) × Z961, +12.19 %) and 43.3
(PSL(2,151) × Z751, +12.40 %) got ~30,000 evaluations in 3.5 h over 2–3 cores without reaching cost 0; every run sat on a
cost-5 plateau (one uncovered α-orbit). On the (15,8) proxy psl 31 × Z601, at a similar median (9–10 units), the same
code solves in ~2,500 evaluations (6/6), so P(0) at full scale is lower than the random-S tail suggested (150
prefiltered samples at μ 41.9: mean 9.4, variance 11, 2 samples at 2 units). Diagnostics: the uncovered elements of
low-cost S have no structure beyond the fixed points (DUMPUNC); targeted moves do not matter for k = 5 (TARGP 0 vs 70:
10/10 solves each on the proxy, same speed); orbit pairs do matter (PAIRS: 1 random pair in 5 gave median 16 units for
every third orbit, against ~9, and it is visible as a level-5 ball ~9,000 short of the usual ~578,900, i.e. short
relations among its letters). Reheats (RESTART = 0) return to the best state, so a run never leaves its first plateau;
a RESTART = 1 run (fresh S after 50 idle steps) and two SL(2,109) × Z991 runs (μ 37.9, +11.57 %) were tried last.

**Lead for other cells.** The k = 5 advantage depends only on λ = W/N being large (fixed points then cost
e^(−λ/5) ≈ 0.03 each) and on a PSL group with a small torus. Every big D = 10 cell has λ ≈ 17–18 at its frontier
(and D = 9 cells ~15–16), so k = 5 with FIXPRE should beat k = 3 on (13,10), (14,10), (16,10) and the like wherever the
orbit method is the tool; check with `klist.py d D LO HI 5` and RANDORB units (FIXPRE=40) before a long run. For
Δ = 15 k = 5 needs f = 0 (15 = 3 × 5); for other Δ, f = Δ mod 5 fixed generators come back (worse, see round 1, c8).

## Method

research/lowmem/README.md: the orbit search of research/structured (S closed under α = conjugation by an element of
order 3 times z ↦ rz), with a new evaluator that needs two N-bit bitsets instead of ~14 bytes per element.

Why this cell needed new code, not a new idea: the size law (research/frontier/SIZE_LAW.md §3.3) predicts that
random-S annealing reaches only ~2.33·10⁹ vertices here (+1 %, "marginal; N ≈ 1.2 G, out of reach on this box"),
and the orbit method, which reached +9 % at (14,10), could not be run: haar_sym needs ~11–14 bytes per element,
~14–17 GB at N = 1.2·10⁹. With rows = cosets of U × Z_n the whole state is two bitsets (0.29–0.31 GB per run) and one
evaluation takes 0.9 s on one core, so four runs fit on this 4-core, 16 GB box at once.

The difficulty of an order is μ = N e^(−W/N) with W = 15(1 + 14² + 14⁴ + 14⁶ + 14⁸) = 22,250,358,075 binding words
(D = 10: words of odd length ≤ 9 from (e,A) must reach all of G on side B). The table value corresponds to μ = 4.5:
even random S nearly works there. Each +1 % in N multiplies μ by about e^(0.19) ≈ 1.2 (d ln μ / d ln N = 1 + W/N ≈ 19).

## What was tried and what did not help

- **Squad round d15D10-r1** (8 Haiku cards; research/squads/LOG.md): search parameters K, TARGP, ORBW, the choice of α,
  fixed generators (f = 3, 6), orbit size k = 5 and screening of ~25 groups by the cost of random symmetric S at full
  scale. Nothing beat the defaults (K 8, TARGP 70, ORBW 1, f 0, k 3). The useful findings were about groups: cyclic
  factors n > 1000 give heavy-tailed costs (mean 100–540 at μ ≈ 20), and big p with tiny n (p ≥ 300) evaluates
  1.5–7.5× slower because the rows get short (p² rows of pn bits). Good shapes are p ≈ 130–200 with n ≈ 100–800.
- **Other evaluators considered and dropped.** Storing one row per α-orbit (rows = cosets of the torus C(g) × Z_n,
  so that α acts by sub-row permutations) needs short sub-rows of p ± 1 bits; by my estimate it would be ~1.5× faster at
  best, worth about +0.2 % in N. A sparse "row-local push" for levels 6–7 would save ~30 % per evaluation. Neither is
  worth the risk with a few hours left: the gain per 10× compute is only ~+2 % in N (evaluations to solve grow like
  e^(μ/4) and d ln μ / d ln N ≈ 19).
