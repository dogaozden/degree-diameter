## Why the cell is easy for orbit-symmetric Haar graphs

For a Haar graph H(G,S) with |S| = 11 and D = 10 the binding condition is that every element of G is reached from
(e,A) by an odd word of length ≤ 9: W = 11·(1 + 10² + 10⁴ + 10⁶ + 10⁸) = 1,111,111,111 words. At the table
(N = |G| = 65.3M) λ = W/N = 17.0 and μ = N·e^(−λ) = 2.7, far below what the orbit search of research/structured
solves at D = 10 ((10,10) at μ = 15, (12,10) at μ = 20). The cell was open only because our copy of the table lacked it.

## What was learned (round 1 and solo runs)

- k = 5 (S = {e} ∪ two α-orbits of size 5) beats k = 3 (S = {e, t} ∪ three orbits of size 3) at D = 10:
  PSL(2,89) × Z211 (μ = 24.1) solved with k = 5 in 609 evaluations, while SL(2,79) × Z151 (μ = 24.5) with k = 3 stopped
  at cost 3 after 8,169 evaluations.
- Large cyclic factors mix badly at D = 10 too. RANDORB medians at μ ≈ 24–33 (k = 5): PSL(2,89) × Z211 33 and
  PSL(2,71) × Z421 41–46, against 95 for PSL(2,41) × Z2161 and 88 for PSL(2,41) × Z2201 (ideal ≈ μ + fixed-point misses
  ≈ 26–34). A crude model: rearrangements of a word that agree in Q also agree in Z_n, so the lost fraction of words
  grows like (number of rearrangements)/|Q|, and μ grows by e^(λ·loss) with λ ≈ 14.5.
