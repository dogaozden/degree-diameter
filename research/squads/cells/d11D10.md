## Why the cell is easy for orbit-symmetric Haar graphs

For a Haar graph H(G,S) with |S| = 11 and D = 10 the binding condition is that every element of G is reached from
(e,A) by an odd word of length ≤ 9: W = 11·(1 + 10² + 10⁴ + 10⁶ + 10⁸) = 1,111,111,111 words. At the table
(N = |G| = 65.3M) λ = W/N = 17.0 and μ = N·e^(−λ) = 2.7, far below what the orbit search of research/structured
solves at D = 10 ((10,10) at μ = 15, (12,10) at μ = 20). The cell was open only because our copy of the table lacked it.

## What was learned (round 1 and solo runs)

- k = 5 (S = {e} ∪ two α-orbits of size 5) beats k = 3 (S = {e, t} ∪ three orbits of size 3) at D = 10:
  PSL(2,89) × Z211 (μ = 24.1) solved with k = 5 in 609 evaluations, while SL(2,79) × Z151 (μ = 24.5) with k = 3 stopped
  at cost 3 after 8,169 evaluations.
- Large cyclic factors mix badly at D = 10 too. RANDORB medians at μ ≈ 24–33 (k = 5): PSL(2,89) × Z211 33 and
  PSL(2,71) × Z421 41–46, against 95 for PSL(2,41) × Z2161 and 88 for PSL(2,41) × Z2201 (ideal ≈ μ + fixed-point misses
  ≈ 26–34). A crude model: rearrangements of a word that agree in Q also agree in Z_n, so the lost fraction of words
  grows like (number of rearrangements)/|Q|, and μ grows by e^(λ·loss) with λ ≈ 14.5.

## Round 2 (2026-10-25): PSL(2,243) × Z11 with an automorphism of order 10

- The target. Every group in (74.4M, 78.9M] that the k = 5 conjugation family offers has |Fix(α)| ≥ 20 and/or a large
  cyclic factor. PSL(2,243) × Z11 (N = 78,917,652, μ = 60.6) is different: the Frobenius of GF(3^5) has order 5 and
  only PSL(2,3) is fixed, and the cyclic factor is tiny. k = 5 with the Frobenius (round-1 lead) reached cost 15
  (3 clumps) in 8,400 evaluations and stalled (Opus run, 48 min).
- The structure that won: α = (conj by an involution c fixed by the Frobenius) ∘ Frobenius on PSL(2,243), times z ↦ r z
  on Z11. α has order 10, S = {e} ∪ one α-orbit of size 10 (haar_sym `ORBIT="10 1 1" AFROB=j AFROBH=… | AFROBD=1`).
  Uncovered elements come in clumps of 10, so μ/10 = 6.1 plus a few misses of elements with short orbits.
  A pure Frobenius orbit is useless here: α⁵ = id on PSL(2,243) leaves only 5 distinct Q-parts in S (cost 71 % of N).
- What mattered, measured with RANDORB (cost of random symmetric S; "clumps" = cost/10):
  - z ↦ 2z (order 10) vs z ↦ 3z (order 5): mean 9.5–10 clumps vs 7.7 (card B7, then Opus: median 74–75 vs 100–111 on
    200 samples each). With z ↦ 3z, α⁵ acts on G as the conjugation by c alone; with z ↦ 2z it also negates z.
    Why this costs words is not settled (B3: no formal word identities up to length 7; the extra coincidences are
    generator-specific).
  - c inner (mat(0,1,2,0), 3 α-fixed elements) vs c = diag(1,−1) outer (1 fixed element): no difference in the bulk
    (A5), slightly fewer small-orbit misses (B7's census: 0.59 vs 1.02 expected).
  - The four Frobenius powers j = 1..4 are inequivalent but indistinguishable in the bulk (A1).
  - Orbit generators with z = 0 put S inside PSL(2,243) × {0} (11 copies of the base graph, cost 71,743,320): `SKIPZ=1`
    (A2).
  - `PREF=1`: test the α-fixed element and the 2-element orbits by meet in the middle (U4 · X5) before the BFS (B1);
    it rejects 47 % of samples at a small fraction of an evaluation; 0.22 s per sample overall (vs 0.45 s).
- The solve: AFROB=1 AFROBD=1 AR=3 SKIPZ=1 PREF=1 PREFMAX=2 RANDB=1 RANDSTOP=1, sample 751 of seed 201 (165 s on one core;
  two other seeds ran alongside). The 200-sample screen of the same α had already shown a cost-1 sample.
- Negative results: the twisted Z11-layer orbit S = {(g^k u^−k, k)} (A8) loses about 31 % of its words; no non-search
  route beats 148.7M (B8: abelian/bi-circulant at most 907,753 per side, blow-ups and lifts far off); the conjugation
  family at 150.65M and 153.3M (A6, 330 s each) reached 1 and 2 clumps.

## Why the cell stops here for these methods

- The method needs a group of order N with an automorphism α of order k ∈ {5, 10, 11}, very few elements in short
  α-orbits, and good mixing (a large non-abelian factor, a small cyclic factor). PSL(2,243) × Z11 is the only such
  group we found between 74.4M and 86M: Frobenius automorphisms of order 5 need GF(p^5), and the next fields give
  |PSL(2,q)| = 32,736 (q = 32, then n ≈ 2,300: bad mixing) or 1.5·10^10 (q = 3,125); SL(2,243) × Z6, PGL(2,243) × Z6
  and Sz(32) × Z3 are 86M–98M (μ ≥ 215, about 22 clumps even with k = 10). Conjugation automorphisms of order 10 fix a
  torus of size (p ± 1)/2, each element missed with probability e^(−λ/10) ≈ 0.25.
- At this structure's measured clump excess (about 1.3), one core-hour of samples covers about μ ≤ 75, i.e. N ≤ 80M:
  less than 1.5 % above what we hold, and no group with the structure exists in that window.
