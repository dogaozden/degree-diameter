## 2. Where more sheets would have to come from

Same-colour pairs are the binding constraint at odd D: a 10-regular bipartite graph with diameter 7 must reach the
whole of its own colour class within distance 6. Within a lift of GH(q) + j matchings (degree q+1+j) by a group of
order m, every walk type that is *guaranteed* for a close pair (hexagon distance ≤ 4, unique geodesic) is: the geodesic,
the j start-jumps and the j end-jumps. That is 2j+1 sheets, exactly the polygon cover. Beating the table needs
(q, j, m) = (8, 1, ≥ 4) [299,592 vertices] or (7, 2, ≥ 6) [235,296 vertices].

First-moment map (log #assignments − expected misses of random voltages; `lift2` initial costs):

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 1 | 4 | 504 | 4 | positive (annealing solves it in seconds) |
| 3 | 1 | 4 | 2,912 | 5 | ≈ 4.3k − 22k < 0 |

So at (10,7) only algebraic structure can work. The diagnostic (`DIAG=64`) shows misses only at even hexagon distance

## 4. New construction: the doubled matching (DM)

Take GH(q), one matching σ, and **double every matching edge** (two parallel edges, voltages 1 and 4 in Z₆). Hexagon
voltages 3ν with ν ∈ {0,1}. Degree q+3, lift 6·|GH(q)| vertices. For q = 7: degree 10, **235,296 > 224,694**.

*Claim (proved by the case analysis below and checked exactly with lift2 at q = 3, 4, 5).* In Z₆ = Z₂ × Z₃ the
Z₃-coordinate of a walk is its jump count. Then for every same-colour pair (v,u):
- start-jumps v → σ(v) → geodesic → u (length ≤ 6) give both sheets of coset 1 (the two parallel copies differ by 3);
- end-jumps give both sheets of coset 2;
- if d(v,u) ≤ 4: the geodesic plus a 2-cycle on v's doubled edge (length d+2 ≤ 6) gives both sheets of coset 0;
- opposite-colour pairs (d ≤ 5) get all six sheets the same way (length ≤ 7).
The only possible misses are sheets {0,3} of **opposite** same-colour pairs (d = 6). Such a pair is covered iff
(A) its q+1 geodesics do not all have the same ν-parity, or (R) a *route* exists: a walk of length ≤ 6 with jump sum
≡ 0 (mod 3) and at least one jump. `DIAG=64` confirms it: with random ν, all misses sit at d = 6, at most one per
target (q = 3: 1.6 %, q = 4: 1.45 %, q = 5: 1.06 % of opposite targets).

`research/d10D7/dmfast.c` computes this cost exactly. It agrees with lift2: q = 3: 1,378 bad pairs × 2 = 2,756;
q = 4: 20,985 × 2 = 41,970. It anneals ν with exact incremental updates (each flag is on 6q⁵ geodesics), and σ by full
route recomputation (small q). Numbers for random (σ, ν):

| q | opposite pairs | covered by routes | bad pairs (random ν) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 88,452 | 87.9 % | 1,378 (1.56 %) |
| 4 | 1,397,760 | 76.2 % | 20,985 (1.50 %) |
| 5 | 12,206,250 | 65.9 % | 129,883 (1.06 %) |
| 7 | 329,551,656 | 50.8 % | 1,266,484 (0.38 %) |

Why it does not close at q = 7:
- **Routes are capped by counting**: a point source has 13,680 route walks of length ≤ 6 at q = 7 (types (+,−) with
  hexagon segments (0,2,0), (0,2,2), (0,4,0), (2,2,0): 9,072; (−,+) with (1,2,1): 3,584; (+,+,+) and (−,−,−): 1,024)
  against 16,807 opposite targets. About 85.7 % of the endpoints are opposite to the source, so even with no collisions
  routes cover ≈ 70 % (at most 81 %); random σ gives 1 − e^(−0.70) ≈ 50 %, matching the measured 50.8 %. At least
  ≈ 19–30 % of opposite pairs (≈ 6–10·10⁷) need Condition A.
- **Condition A alone looks unsatisfiable**: annealing ν with no routes bottoms out at 72 of 2,016 pairs at q = 2 (two
  seeds, same value) and ≈ 7.2 % of pairs at q = 3 (random: 25 % and 12.5 %). It is "avoid the union of
  codimension-q subspaces" of the q⁶-dimensional cohomology H¹(GH(q); Z₂) (= the Steinberg module; apartments through
  a fixed chamber form a basis), and these subspaces appear to cover it.
- **Annealing**: ν alone at q = 4: 20,985 → 12,166; jointly (ν and σ) at q = 3: 1,378 → 253 (two seeds: 253, 256),
  even though the first moment is slightly positive there.
- Symmetric voltages do not help. A voltage invariant under a Borel subgroup (any function of the Weyl distance to a
  fixed chamber) is cohomologous to the indicator of that single chamber, because all basis apartments get the same
  holonomy. Voltages defined by incidence with hyperplanes are coboundaries: 100 % failure, checked at q = 5.

### 9.1 The joint algebraic cover

The exp2-squad's exact cover for one freed flag (p, l = M(p)): any M with M(S'(p)) = S(l), where S'(p) = points at
distance 4 from p not via l and S(l) = lines at distance 4 from l not via p (q⁴ each). Every point opposite p lies on
exactly one line of S(l), and every line opposite l contains exactly one point of S'(p), so both freed sources are
covered with no search. For K freed flags, give every point u the membership vector (bit k set iff u ∈ S'(p_k)) and
every line L the vector (bit k iff L ∈ S(l_k)). Any M that maps every point to a line with the **same** vector covers
all K pairs at once. Such an M exists iff, for every vector, #points = #lines; the defect is the mismatch
Σ_v |#points(v) − #lines(v)|.
- Two opposite flags always have mismatch 0. This is the self-duality of the counts for opposite chambers in a
  generalized hexagon of order (q, q): the counts depend only on the Weyl distance w₀ and the parameters, and w₀ is
  fixed by the diagram automorphism. Flags that are not opposite do not have this property.
- K = 3: about 5 % of random opposite triples have mismatch 0 (squad c1). K = 4: one zero quadruple in 10⁵ samples
  (c1: [[22726,35449],[29552,37145],[25992,30647],[2625,21254]]). No zero at K = 5. For K = 12 the mismatch is about
  1,500–3,000; local search over the flag set gains only about 1 % (c2), and H(2) subhexagon flags do not help (c1).
- In practice the mismatched leftovers are repaired by the proxy search: K = 12 solves in 86 s from an initial unmet
  count of about 10⁴. The exp2 install (covers installed pair by pair, conflicts locked) starts near 9·10⁴ for K = 21.
  **(10,7) ≥ 224,718** (K = 12) is apsp VALID.

### 9.3 Dualities: (11,7) and (12,7)

For q = 3^e the hexagon H(q) is self-dual. If φ is a duality (points → lines → points, preserving incidence) with
φ(p) = l and φ(l) = p for a flag (p, l), then φ maps S'(p) onto S(l). So M = φ gives the exact cover for **every**
absolute flag at once, with no mismatch. `research/d10D7/dual/dualfind.c`:
1. finds a polarity θ of the subhexagon H(3) inside H(9) (the points with GF(3) coordinates): fix an apartment and a
   type-swapping reflection of it, propagate along edges using distance profiles to a frame, and branch when the

2. extends θ to a duality φ of H(9) (two candidates; the Frobenius picks one). φ² fixes exactly the 728 elements of

### 9.4 The B2 condition (the main result of the session)

Watching the q = 9 growth add pairs with zero uncovered targets led to a much simpler sufficient condition than the
exp2 type-4 cover. **If M maps B2(p) = the q(q+1) points at distance 2 from p onto B2(l) = the q(q+1) lines at
distance 2 from l, then the freed pair (p, l) needs nothing else.** Every point x is within distance 3 of some line at
distance 2 from l: if d(x, l) = 5, take the second line on the geodesic; if d(x, l) ≤ 3, take a line through x or
through the foot of x on l. So (p,0) → (L,0) → (u,0) → (M(u),1) followed by ≤ 3 hexagon steps reaches every point
of level 1 within 6. Dually, every line is within distance 3 of a point at distance 2 from p, so (l,0) reaches every
line of level −1. p and l need not be incident.
- Freed points must not be collinear (each one's own extra edges are gone, and it would sit in another's B2).
- B2 sets of different pairs must be consistent. Two points at distance 4 share exactly one B2 point (their midpoint
  w), so their lines must be at distance 4 with M(w) = the midline. Two opposite points share none, so their lines
  must be opposite as well, or the midline of the lines would need a preimage in two disjoint sets. A duality (q = 3^e)
  satisfies all of this for every set of non-collinear points. For other q the safe choice is pairwise opposite points
  and pairwise opposite lines, paired in any way.
- K is then the size of greedy maximal partial ovoids/spreads: 67/68 at q = 8, 56/57 at q = 7, 39/32 at q = 5; and
  2,821 non-collinear points at q = 9 (Hoffman bound 6,643).
- Same-side hubs must be within 6 of each other. For opposite freed points this has to go through the hub graph
  (degree ≤ q−1, or ≤ 7 in the 5-level case). With 4K = 11,284 hubs in the 5-level (12,7) graph a random hub graph
  leaves some pairs at distance 8, so the 675,584 candidate failed (diameter 8). A repair by 2-switches
  (research/d10D7/dual/hubfix.c) fixes smaller hub graphs.
`d7hex b2` (opposite sets, optionally from files B2PTS/B2LINES) and `d7hex b2dual` (duality M, non-collinear set)
build the solutions; research/d10D7/dual/x5gen.c builds the 5-level graphs.
