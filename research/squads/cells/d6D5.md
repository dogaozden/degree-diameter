## 3. This run

### 3.1 A counting lemma: abelian groups are out
If G is abelian and |S| = 6, every element of (S−S)+(S−S) is a − b + c − d with a, b, c, d ∈ S. If the multisets
{a, c} and {b, d} meet, it reduces to x − y (at most 31 values with 0). Otherwise it is determined by a pair of
disjoint 2-multisets: 6·15 + 15·10 = 240 choices. So |(S−S)+(S−S)| ≤ 271, and **an abelian Haar graph of degree 6
and diameter 5 has at most 2·271 = 542 vertices**. Every abelian group of order ≥ 272 is excluded (this matches the
orbit scans: cyclic groups of order 343 have costs 258–910). The same count for general Δ gives
1 + Δ(Δ−1) + Δ·C(Δ,2) + C(Δ,2)·C(Δ−1,2).


**Generalized dihedral groups.** Let G = A⋊⟨τ⟩ with A abelian and τ inverting A, and write S = S₀ ∪ S₁τ (k rotations,
6 − k reflections). The quotients x⁻¹y are rotations in R = (S₀−S₀) ∪ (S₁−S₁) (for two reflections,
(bτ)⁻¹(b'τ) = b − b') and reflections (b − a)τ. A rotation in (S⁻¹S)² is r + r' (r, r' ∈ R) or
(fτ)(f'τ) = f − f' = (b − b') + (a' − a) ∈ (S₁−S₁) + (S₀−S₀). So the rotation part lies in R + R, of size at most
g(k) + g(6−k) + (k(k−1)+1)((6−k)(5−k)+1) with g(d) the abelian count (g = 1, 5, 19, 55, 131, 271 for d = 1..6); for
k = 1 or 5 the cross term is already inside (S₁−S₁)+(S₁−S₁), so the maximum is g(5) = 131 (k = 1, 5), against 99 for
k = 2, 4 and 87 for k = 3 (k = 0, 6 leave the reflections uncovered). **So a generalized dihedral Haar graph of degree 6
and diameter 5 has |A| ≤ 131, i.e. at most 524 vertices.** Hill-climbing in D₁₀₀₉ and D₁₇₃ (tools/dihcheck.c) reaches
exactly 131 and never more. This explains D346's exhaustive minimum (160 uncovered ≥ 173 − 131).

### 3.2 Exhaustive Haar search (orderly), `research/fresh65/tools/haarex2.c`
H(G,S) ≅ H(G, φ(gSh)) ≅ H(G, S⁻¹). haarex2 fixes e ∈ S, takes the first two other elements (r, r₂) as the minimum
of their Aut-class of ordered pairs, and prunes a prefix as soon as some translate P t⁻¹, t⁻¹P, P⁻¹t, tP⁻¹ (t ∈ P)
has a pair in a smaller class, so every isomorphism class of Haar graphs keeps a representative. It then tests
(S⁻¹S)² = G and (SS⁻¹)² = G with bitsets. Validated against the plain version haarex.c (Heis(7): same minimum,
123,366,620 vs 6,803,423 sets) and against apsp (order 200: VALID sets agree).

**Order 343 (686 vertices) is impossible for Haar graphs.** The three abelian groups fall to §3.1. Heis(7) =
SmallGroup(343,3): no S covers even the A side (at least 4 elements always missed). C49⋊C7 = SmallGroup(343,4):
29 classes of S cover the A side, none covers the B side. So a Haar graph beating 684 needs |G| ≥ 344.

## 4. Conclusion: why the cell does not fall to these methods

1. **Counting.** A (6,5) graph on ≥ 686 vertices needs ≥ 343 vertices on a side, each vertex reaching all of its side
   by at most 781 walks of length ≤ 4 (redundancy ≤ 2.3; a random-like structure leaves ≈ 35 uncovered). Only
   structures that put the word collisions on already-covered vertices can work.
2. **Geometry is too small at Δ = 6.** GQ level graphs give 510, buildings 630; GH quotients fail by counting, and
   +56 vertices of surgery on the 630 graph (31 % of its same-side pairs have a unique short walk) stalled at 5,340.
3. **Haar graphs stop at exactly 684 for |G| ≤ 351** (§3.4): abelian groups ≤ 271 and generalized dihedral groups
   ≤ 262 by counting (§3.1), the 41 non-abelian groups of order 343–351 by exhaustive search (two independent
   implementations for order 343), and 684 itself is the Haar graph of C6×(C19⋊C3). The "cost 2" wall that every annealer
   met is the true optimum of the best groups. For |G| ≥ 352 the covering gets harder (μ ≈ 38 at 351, 41 at 360, 47 at 375),
   and the low-involution groups of 352–360 that finished are negative too.
4. **Lifts do not escape.** Side-transitive symmetric lifts are Haar graphs; free 2+2/3+3 lifts are 4× worse than the
   Haar optimum after 60 s and cannot be improved from it; larger bases are worse still (c2, c3, c16: cost grows with h).
5. **Everything else is worse**: coset graphs of almost-simple groups (13), of products (35), non-split (24); orbit
   invariance under non-cyclic groups (52); inverse-closed S (one condition, but more collisions); loops; algebraic F7³
   graphs; two-sided Cayley graphs (left and right letters commute, so interleavings of a walk collapse to one vertex);
   structure-free or seeded edge-swap annealing; +2 surgery on the 684 graph (no removable edge).

What is left untried, and would need different tools: exact search (SAT/ILP with strong symmetry breaking) over lifts
with h = 2 or 3, where the voltage space (≈ |Γ|⁹ ≈ 10²⁰) is far beyond enumeration; Haar graphs of the 260 groups of order
352–360 with many involutions (expected worse); constructions with no group acting with few orbits.

**Best result of this run: no new record; (6,5) stays at 684.** New theorems: the abelian bound 271, the generalized
dihedral bound 131, and "no Haar graph at (6,5) with 343 ≤ |G| ≤ 351", with 684 attained by a Haar graph.
