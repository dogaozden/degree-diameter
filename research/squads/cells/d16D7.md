## The construction

Five levels j ∈ Z5, each a copy of the incidence graph I of the split Cayley hexagon H(13) (order (13,13), 402,234
points and 402,234 lines), with a TYPE: levels 0, 2, 4 are H, levels 1, 3 are the dual hexagon H^D. H^D has the same
incidence graph; only the names change: in an H level the "points" (class A) are the points of H, in an H^D level the
"points" are the lines of H. Every level gets the incidence edges. Matchings, k = 1, 2:

    M_k^(j): A_j -> B_{j+k} (a bijection), edge (a, j) ~ (M_k^(j)(a), j+k).

So every vertex has degree 14 + 2 = 16. This is the table's 5-copy construction except that some copies are dualized.

**Base graph.** For ANY bijections the diameter is 7: two levels differ by ±1 or ±2, and levels j, j+k with the
matching M_k^(j) are exactly the three-copy situation (research/d13D7/REPORT.md §2), whose proof only uses that each level
is a generalized hexagon of order (q, q). (Same argument as the table construction.)

**Friendly transitions.** A transition (j, k) is friendly when the types of j and j+k differ. Then A_j and B_{j+k} are
the same kind of element of H (both points, or both lines), and we take M_k^(j) = identity. With types HDHDH the friendly
transitions are 0→1, 1→2, 2→3, 3→4 (k = 1) and 3→0, 4→1 (k = 2); the other four (4→0, 0→2, 2→4 and 1→3) are not, and
use any bijection (we take a random G-equivariant one, see below).

**Surgery theorem (one transition at a time).** Fix a transition j → j+k with matching σ: A_j → B_{j+k} and a set R ⊂ A_j.
Delete the edges (r, j) ~ (σ(r), j+k), r ∈ R. Suppose
- (H1) R is non-collinear in level j (no two elements of R at distance 2);
- (H2) σ(R) is non-concurrent in level j+k;
- (H3) σ(Γ₂(r)) = Γ₂(σ(r)) for every r ∈ R (Γ₂ = elements at distance 2 in I).
Then every pair of vertices in levels j and j+k is still within distance 7, using only these two levels.
*Proof* (as in REPORT.md §2, with the hub cases replaced; a = A-element, b = B-element of a level):
1. a ∉ R in level j: (a,j) → (σ(a), j+k) → anything in level j+k within 6 more (≤ 7).
2. b in level j, a′ in level j+k: pick a B-element m ∋ a′ of level j+k not in σ(R) (H2: at most one of the q+1 is);
   (b,j) → (σ⁻¹(m), j) [≤ 5] → (m, j+k) → (a′, j+k): ≤ 7.
3. b in level j, b′ ∉ σ(R) in level j+k: (b,j) → (σ⁻¹(b′), j) [≤ 5] → (b′, j+k): ≤ 6.
4. b in level j, b′ = σ(r): (σ(r), j+k) → y → L′ ∈ Γ₂(σ(r)) → (σ⁻¹(L′), j) ∈ Γ₂(r) (H3; not in R by H1, so its edge
   exists) → b within 3 more for a suitable L′ (every B-element is within distance 3 of some element of Γ₂(r): the
   element of Γ₂(r) on a geodesic): ≤ 6.
5. r ∈ R, a′ in level j+k: (r,j) → (u,j), u ∈ Γ₂(r) [2] → (σ(u), j+k) ∈ Γ₂(σ(r)) [3] → a′ within 3 more (covering
   lemma: every A-element is within distance 3 of some element of Γ₂(ℓ)): ≤ 6.
6. r ∈ R, b′ in level j+k: the same route, then b′ within 4 more of σ(u) (every B-element is within distance 4 of some
   element of Γ₂(σ(r))): ≤ 7.  ∎

For a friendly transition σ is the identity and Γ₂ is the same set on both sides, so **(H3) holds at every element** and
(H2) is (H1). So: *any non-collinear set of points of H(13) (or non-concurrent set of lines) can be freed at a friendly
transition.* The largest such sets are bounded by q⁴ + q² + 1 = 28,731 (Hoffman), against at most q³ + 1 = 2,198 for the
pairwise opposite sets that the non-friendly (H → H) transitions need (the 4,025,508 construction freed 792 of those).

**Hubs.** A deleted edge frees one A-slot at (r, j) and one B-slot at (σ(r), j+k). A line-side hub (class B) is joined
to one free A-slot in EVERY level, a point-side hub (class A) to one free B-slot in every level (5 edges each). A hub
then reaches all of level j through its level-j neighbour within 1 + 6; hubs of opposite sides are within 1 + 5 + 1 = 7;
same-side hubs are joined by a hub graph (line-side ~ point-side, degree ≤ 16 − 5 = 11) with all same-side distances ≤ 6.
The number of hubs per side is the minimum over the levels of the free slots of that class. With types HDHDH every level
has exactly one friendly outgoing transition except level 3 (two), and one friendly incoming except level 1 (two):
level 0: out 0→1 (R_H), in 3→0 (R_L); level 1: out 1→2 (R_L), in 0→1, 4→1 (2·R_H); level 2: out 2→3 (R_H), in 1→2 (R_L);
level 3: out 3→4, 3→0 (2·R_L), in 2→3 (R_H); level 4: out 4→1 (R_H), in 3→4 (R_L). So both sides get min(|R_H|, |R_L|)
hubs and

    n = 10·402,234 + 2·min(|R_H|, |R_L|),

R_H a non-collinear point set and R_L a non-concurrent line set of H(13). (Five levels and two matchings form the
regular tournament on 5 vertices; a 3H/2D split is the largest cut, 6 of 10 transitions, so one friendly transition per
level is the most there is. Three levels (Δ = q+2) always leave a level with no friendly outgoing transition, which is
why the cell-13-7 report found the dual copy useless there.)

**Non-friendly transitions.** On 4→0, 0→2, 2→4 (H → H) and 1→3 (H^D → H^D) the surgery needs the original
local-duality hypotheses: R pairwise opposite, σ(R) pairwise opposite, σ a local duality on each Γ₂(r) (cell-13-7 /
cell-10-7 construction). These add s orbits to both minima. Under the order-61 group below, the largest invariant set of
pairwise opposite points has 2 orbits (popp.c, exact max clique of the orbit compatibility graph; lines: 4 orbits), so the
non-friendly surgery adds 2·2·61 = 244 vertices: n = 10·402,234 + 2·61·(min(K_H, K_L) + 2) with K_H, K_L the orbit
counts of R_H, R_L. typed5s builds σ equivariantly (Γ₂(r) → Γ₂(σ(r)) by a random bijection, extended by G) and its
`edges` mode re-checks (H1)–(H3) for every freed element.

**Other cells.** The same typed levels apply wherever the table uses 2r+1 ≥ 5 copies of a non-self-dual hexagon: (14,7)
= 5·|I(H(11))| (Δ = q+3) and (15,7) = 7·|I(H(11))| (Δ = q+4). For (14,7) the count is 10·177,156 + 2·min(|R_H|, |R_L|)
with non-collinear sets of H(11) (Hoffman bound 14,763), i.e. roughly +17,000 instead of the current +3,024 (756
pairwise opposite points); the hub graph then needs degree ≤ 9 with same-side distances ≤ 6, which random voltage graphs
give at that size. For seven levels (3 matchings) the types must be chosen so that every level keeps a friendly outgoing
and a friendly incoming transition. Not done here (other jobs own those cells).

## Symmetry and the check

At 4M vertices a plain all-pairs BFS costs about 44 core-hours, so the graph is made invariant under G = ⟨g⟩, g of order
61 in SL(3,13) ⊂ G2(13) acting as (v, a, w) ↦ (gv, a, g⁻ᵀw) on the quadric X0X4 + X1X5 + X2X6 = X3² (collin.py checks it
maps all 402,234 lines to lines; it acts freely: 6,594 orbits of 61 on points and on lines). R_H and R_L are unions of
G-orbits, the non-friendly bijections are G-equivariant (orbit to orbit with an offset), hubs come in G-orbits and the hub
graph is a voltage graph over Z61. Then (x, j) ↦ (gx, j), hub (k, t) ↦ (k, t+1) is an automorphism; apsp_aut.c checks it
edge by edge and runs the bit-parallel BFS from one vertex per orbit (n/61 sources), which gives the exact diameter.
