## How (details and proofs: research/d12D5/EDGECHART.md)

Route-chart lift: a 4-regular bipartite controller C (m + m vertices) and fibre F_3^4; a controller edge with letter
(A, b) gives the lift edges (a, x) ~ (b', A x + λ b), λ ∈ F_3, so the lift is 12-regular on 2·81·m vertices. A same-side
pair of the lift is within distance 4 if its controller pair is joined by a nonsingular nonbacktracking 4-walk, or is
at distance 2 with a complete pencil, or is a diagonal pair covered otherwise; then the diameter is ≤ 5.
The controller is a voltage lift of K_{3,3} + a doubled perfect matching (3 + 3 vertices, 12 edges) by a cyclic group.

Three steps took the Oct 24 construction (colour charts, m = 60) to m = 75:
1. **Per-edge charts.** Every base edge gets its own letter (12 letters instead of 4 colours). Walk singularity then
   depends on the base walk, and joint annealing of voltages and letters reached m = 66.
2. **Free diagonals.** The last uncovered pairs at m = 69 were the six diagonal orbits (u, u). Closed backtracking walks
   of length ≤ 4 reach x + ⟨c_e, c_f⟩ and x + ⟨c_e, A_e⁻¹b_l⟩ (18 planes per vertex); if they cover F_3^4 the diagonal
   pairs are free and no 4-cycles are needed. With all diagonals free, the controller ceiling ("fake" chart with every
   walk good) on K33m rises from m = 66 (cost 2 at 72) to m = 81.
3. **Backtrack-complete charts by construction** (annealing never found one). In a frame gauge, completeness of a
   vertex is a covering problem for 4 "leg triangles" in PG(2,3) (1,920 solutions), and each edge needs a
   collineation linking the leg triangles at its two ends; a small CSP solves it (research/d12D5/diag/). These charts
   have only 48 singular walks of 648 (8 per vertex, the minimum among complete vertices), no pencil failure and no
   missing diagonal point. Held fixed: m = 69. With per-edge letter swaps among variants that share the leg triangles
   (so completeness is kept): m = 72 and 75.

## What failed, and why

- No colour chart and no per-edge chart over F_3 makes every 4-walk nonsingular (universal3.py for colours; for
  per-edge charts, an A-perfect vertex forces its B-neighbours' walks to be singular, EDGECHART.md).
- Joint annealing with free chart moves destroys backtrack-completeness (m = 72–78 runs ended at cost 10–18).
- Forcing 4-cycles by involution digons (DIGON=1) is much worse (fake cost 116 at m = 72).
- Non-cyclic groups: worse than cyclic at every order tried (orders 24–27; squad rounds 1–2).
- Other bases: other k = 3 bases, k = 2, k = 4 and random k = 5..16 bases all have lower controller ceilings than K33m.
- Haar (single-orbit) controllers: exhaustive over 163 groups of order 66–84 and 120 chart variants, minimum cost 2
  (a central involution stays uncovered on each side).
- The sufficient criterion is not the obstacle: an exact lift-coverage checker (squad round 1, c8) agrees with it on
  every near miss.
- Even orders suffer from the involution (fake ceiling Z_26: cost 4, Z_28: cost 4), odd orders do not (Z_25, Z_27: 0).

## Ceiling and next steps

K33m over Z_27 (m = 81, 13,122 vertices) is within the controller ceiling (fake cost 0) and real runs reach cost 2
(one orbit pair). Z_29 (m = 87) has fake cost 12, so K33m tops out near m = 81 with this coverage criterion.
Going further needs a controller family with a higher ceiling or more coverage (e.g. unions of singular walks).
The same backtrack-complete trick may help other F_3 route-chart cells ((9,5), (15,5)); over F_4 it is impossible
(the 18 planes cover at most 70 of the 85 points of PG(3,4)).
