**Result.** The table value 4,650,100 is Gómez's ΩH₉ (thesis UPC 1986, §2.9.3, pp. 43–46), and ΩH₉ has diameter
exactly 9 (10 if all blocks have one type), under both readings of the thesis figures. So the entry is not a valid
(12,8) graph; nor are the other D = 8 values of the thesis (ΩH₅, ΩH₇, ΩH₈, ΩH₁₁, ΩH₁₃: a K₁-vertex has eccentricity 9
in each). No valid graph above 4,650,100 was found. The best valid (12,8) graph we know stays the Haar graph on
PSL(2,67) × Z₁₃, 3,909,048 vertices (round 2 of cell-12-8).

**What I did**
- Read §2.8–2.9 of the thesis myself (scanned PDF). The labels (§2.8.1, §2.8.9) join a copy of K₁ with label t to
  vertices with label t in every copy. The figures leave open which colour class the inner and outer dots are; the
  thesis's own drawing convention gives a reading (reading 1) different from the Oct 25 one (reading 2): there a
  K₁-vertex is on line t in (a)-blocks and on point t in (b)-blocks.
- Built H(q) independently (Q(6,q) with Tits' equations, q = 3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 11, 13; each checked by all-pairs BFS:
  Moore count + diameter 6 ⇒ generalized hexagon).
- Built ΩH₉ in both readings and computed the exact diameter with a new checker, `apsp_auto.c`: BFS from one vertex
  per orbit of a group generated by explicit automorphisms (SL(3,9) elements of G₂(9), the Z₅ rotation, block swaps),
  each checked edge by edge. Reading 2: 18 orbits, diameter 9, 18 s. Reading 1: 4,380 orbits, diameter 9, 39 s.
  Witness pairs in `research/d12D8/omega/witness_pairs.txt`, re-checked with a plain BFS.
- Proved it for every reading: Theorem 1 (one label per K₁-vertex: distance D₁ + 3) and Theorem 2 (two labels, any
  pairing, any block mix, q ≥ 7: a counting bound, 39,763 < 59,049 reachable opposite lines at q = 9).
- Ran the other thesis values with an edge-list-free BFS (`omega_bfs.c`), up to ΩH₁₃ with 36,201,060 vertices.

**Beating 4,650,100: tried, and why it failed**
- Repairing ΩH₉ by relabelling copies: automorphism/duality relabellings change nothing (Theorem 1); random
  relabellings leave ~22,000 vertices at distance 9 from every K₁-vertex (down from 1,062,882), ~10¹⁰ far pairs.
- Hexagon level graphs at D = 8: two transitions per route at most, so the levels form a 2r-regular graph of diameter
  2; ≤ 15 levels at q = 9, i.e. ≤ 1,992,900 vertices.
- New: duality-hop lifts (levels of H(9), two hop edges x ~ δx per point with per-vertex level voltages, `duallift.c`).
  Random voltages work to L ≈ 10–12 levels (1.33–1.59M); at L = 35 (4,650,100) ~9,500 far vertices per source. The far
  pairs are point–line pairs at hexagon distance 5, which see only ~190 level values (the length-5 geodesic is unique).
- Haar graphs (first-moment ceiling 4,529,596 < 4,650,100; wall ≈ 3.9–4.1M), lifts of H(q) + hops (≤ 2.3M), route-chart
  lifts (the controller would have to beat or match the (3,8), (4,8) or (6,8) records): all below the target, numbers
  in d12D8_table.md §5.
