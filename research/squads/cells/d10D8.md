## 2. A lemma: ten levels for free

Let B be H(q) (or any bipartite graph of diameter 6, so point–line distances are ≤ 5) plus two perfect matchings
σ1, σ2 from points to lines, chosen arbitrarily. Lift B over Z10: hexagon edges keep the level, the edge p–σ1(p)
raises the level by 1 and p–σ2(p) by 4 (seen from the point). Then the lift has diameter ≤ 8, degree q+3 and
10·|H(q)| vertices.

Proof sketch (from a point p at level 0; lines are symmetric). Routes with at most two matching edges, where every
hexagon segment is a geodesic of length ≤ 5 (point–line) or ≤ 6 (same type):
- to a line y: level 0 (p⇝y), +a (p→σ(p)⇝y), −a (p⇝σ(x)→x→y with x on y), a+b (p→σ_a(p)⇝σ_b⁻¹(y)→y), a−b with
  a ≠ b (p→σ_a(p)→σ_b⁻¹σ_a(p)⇝y), all of length ≤ 7. With shifts {1, 4} these are 0, ±1, ±4, 2, 8, 5, ±3: all of Z10.
- to a point x: every ±a ± b is available within 8 (the routes above plus p⇝l→σ⁻¹(l)⇝σ_b(x)→x and
  p⇝l→σ_a⁻¹(l)→σ_bσ_a⁻¹(l)⇝x with l through p), which covers Z10 as well.

Checked by research/d10D8/hex/lift3 (all sources) for random σ1, σ2 at q = 2, 3, 4. This is 10/30 of the table:
the table construction gets another factor of about (q+5)/4 from somewhere, presumably voltages on the hexagon edges.

## 3. Why searching for hexagon lifts at q = 7 is hopeless without algebra

A lift of B over a group of order c has |V(B)| = 39,216 source orbits, not one. For each (source, opposite target,
sheet) there are on average W/(19,608·c) walks of length ≤ 7 (W = 10·9⁶ + … = 5,380,840), i.e. λ ≈ 9.0 at
c = 30. With voltages that behave randomly the number of uncovered triples is about
39,216 · 19,608 · 30 · e^(−9.0) ≈ 3·10⁶, and the first moment (30^196,000 assignments against e^(−3·10⁶)) says such
lifts essentially do not exist. A Haar graph of the same order has one source orbit and μ = N e^(−λ) ≈ 63 at the
table's size. So the table's hexagon construction must be algebraic, and annealing can only work at small q (where
the counts are small: at q = 3 random Z4 × Z5 voltages miss only 4–6 triples).

## 3b. Guaranteed walks in H(q) + matchings (design view of the hexagon family)

Let σ1, σ2: P → L be arbitrary bijections (matching t raises the level by a_t from the point). Routes from a point p
to a line y of length ≤ 7 whose hexagon segments connect arbitrary endpoints (so they exist for every p, y):
H (p⇝y) 1; U_t H (p→σ_t(p)⇝y) 1 each; H U_t (p⇝σ_t⁻¹(y)→y) 1 each; h D_t H (p→l→σ_t⁻¹(l)⇝y, l through p) q+1 each;
H D_t h (p⇝σ_t(x)→x→y, x on y) q+1 each; U_s H U_t 4; U_s D_t H (s ≠ t) 2; H D_s U_t (s ≠ t) 2. That is
4q + 17 walks (45 at q = 7) with two matchings and 2q + 6 with one (the routes U_t H and H U_t have q+1 walks
instead of 1 when the segment joins opposite elements, i.e. for most pairs). The table's 30 levels at q = 7 use about
two thirds of this worst-case budget, so a better design is not excluded by counting; a design is a voltage
assignment for which these walks spread over the whole lift group for every (p, y) (and the dual and same-side
cases). With hexagon voltages 0 the q+1-families are {−g_t(σ_t⁻¹(l)) : l ∋ p} and {−g_t(x) : x ∈ y}; making them
the same set for every p (resp. y) needs a partition of P into ovoids, which H(q) has only for q = 3^e. So at q = 7
any such design must use the hexagon voltages.
A second route to (10,8): H(8) (|H(8)| = 74,898) + one matching has Δ = 10 = q+2 and 2q + 6 = 22 guaranteed walks
per pair; a design with 16 levels would give 1,198,368 > 1,176,480. (The table's Δ = q+2 multiplier at q = 11 is 15.)
None of this was found in this session (squad rounds 1–3 searched small q; see LOG.md).

## 5. The abelian tax of Q × Z_n with orbit-symmetric S (ZOPT, research/d10D8/zopt/)

In G = Q × Z_n with α = (conjugation of order 3) × (z ↦ r z), the letter i of orbit j has Z-part r^i z_j, so a
binding word's Z-part is c(w)·z with a coefficient vector c(w) ∈ Z_n³ that depends only on the word's letter pattern.
Consequences:
- For fixed Q-parts the cost of every offset vector z can be computed from the fibres
  C_q = {c(w) : Q-part of w = q}; only the projective points of PG(2, n) matter (scaling z gives an isomorphic graph).
  `haar_zopt` (haar_sym + ZOPT mode) does this exhaustively: for the best set of seed 3 (cost 9) all 9,507 points of
  PG(2,97) were tried and the run's own z is the optimum (cost 9). So z-polishing does not help; the annealer already
  optimises z.
- Two words with equal c and equal Q-part collide for every z. Only 31,204 distinct c occur among the 5,380,840
  binding words, and they are concentrated (effective number about 7,500), so even generic Q-parts lose
  (pairs sharing c)/|Q| = 5.9 % of the words for PSL(2,23) × Z97 (3.7 % for PSL(2,27) × Z61). Measured duplicate
  rates of random orbit sets: 7.6–23 % (psl 23 × 97), 6.8–10.6 % (psl 27 × 61), 23–27 % (psl 17 × 241); the best set
  found so far has 8.75 %. A loss δ multiplies μ by about e^(δλ): 1.7–2.2 here, which matches the RANDORB medians
  (138 against μ = 63.5).
- The tax is smallest for n around 20–40 (n³ comparable to the number of coefficient classes): very small n
  collapses the classes (n = 7: ≤ 343 classes, ~9 %), large n makes |Q| small. No group of that shape lies in the
  window: SL(2,27) × Z31 (609,336) has the right shape but μ = 89, which cancels the gain.
- Plain random S (no orbits) also has a ~1.5× excess (RANDSAMPLE medians: 97 at μ 63.5 for psl 23 × 97, 145 at
  μ 93.7 for psl 107), so the orbit structure adds about 1.3–1.4× on top.
PSL(2,7) × SL(2,8) × Z7 (592,704, μ 67.6) has the same RANDORB p1 (51) as psl 23 × 97 and a better p10 and median
(76/129 against 84/138); it runs as the fourth search.

improving it, and every stall starts a fresh chain; each finished chain prints `CHAIN n min C secs s evals e S <set>`.

### 8.2 Inverse-closed S (negative)

If S = S⁻¹, the odd words are closed under inversion, so uncovered elements would come in ⟨α, x ↦ x⁻¹⟩-orbits of 6
and μ_A would halve (≈ 11 instead of ≈ 22). With Δ = 10 and α of order 3 the only shape is
S = {e} ∪ O ∪ O⁻¹ ∪ (an α-orbit of involutions) (`INVPAIR=1` in haar_sym2). Measured RANDORB (1000 samples):
minimum cost 154,488 on PSL(2,23) × Z97 and 5,888 on PSL(2,7) × SL(2,8) × Z7, against 35–36 for the usual orbits.
Reason: with e ∈ S and S = S⁻¹ the three length-3 words a·e⁻¹·c, e·(a⁻¹)⁻¹·c and a·(c⁻¹)⁻¹·e are the same element a c, and the
same happens at every length (about half of all words contain e). Dropping e needs S = O ∪ O⁻¹ ∪ I ∪ {u} with u an
α-fixed involution: PSL(2,7) × SL(2,8) × Z7 has |C(α)| = 27 (odd, no such u); PSL(2,23) × Z97 has one, but with a
single free orbit the Z97-part of every word is c(w)·z for one scalar z and c(w) is concentrated on a few residues;
groups with many involutions pay e^(−λ/2) per involution orbit. No group in the window fits.

### 8.3 Exhaustive best response (`EXDESC=1`)

Replacing one orbit slot by its best response over all 197,557 α-orbits takes 85 s (early abort at the current cost).
The cost-7 state of seed 1 is an exact local optimum: no single-orbit replacement in any of the three slots improves it.
From random orbits the exhaustive descent went 133 → 24 → 15 and stopped there (a local optimum after 2 sweeps, 658 s on a
loaded machine). Annealing reaches the same depth (median 17) in about 45 s, so exhaustive best responses buy nothing
per CPU-second. Crossovers of 20 good states (squad r4-c3: two orbits of one state and one of another, 3,420 sets)
are single-orbit replacements of a good state and are all worse (best 24, median 99): good states are isolated
points, and the search can only draw them anew.

### 8.4 Twisted pairing S⁻¹ = β(S) (negative)

`INVPAIR=2`: β = conjugation by mat(0,1,4,0) (det 3, a non-square: an outer involution of PSL(2,7) commuting with α),
trivial on SL(2,8) and Z7; S = {e} ∪ O ∪ ι(O) ∪ (α-orbit of ι-fixed elements) with ι(x) = β(x)⁻¹. Then S⁻¹ = β(S), the
odd words are ι-invariant and there is no e-collapse (S is not inverse-closed). The uncovered set comes in orbits of 6,
but: 1,792 ι-fixed elements (597 orbits, e^(−λ/2) each), one free z in Z7, and an orbit space of only ≈ 1.2·10⁸.
RANDORB: min 69, p1 109, median 1,050 (standard orbits: 35, 50, 128); 4.5 minutes of fresh descents: minima 36–48,
i.e. 6–8 paired units, against 4–8 units (cost 12–24) for the standard family. Not better.
