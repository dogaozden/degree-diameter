## 1. The construction

Main graph (664,300): five levels i ∈ Z5 of the incidence graph of H(9); (p,i) ~ (φ(p),i+1) and (p,i) ~ (φ(p),i+2)
for every non-freed point p, where φ is a duality of H(9) (q = 3², research/d10D7/dual/dualfind.c). This is the table's
graph when nothing is freed. A freed point p loses its 10 matching edges, so (p,i) and (φ(p),i) get 2 free slots each.

Freed set F = the 7,300 points on the 730 lines of a Hermitian spread (pairwise opposite lines; the H(9)-lines in a
hyperplane with elliptic section, research/d10D7/sets/hspread.py). The freed lines φ(F) are the 10 lines through each
point φ(L) of an ovoid φ(S).

Hubs: 14,600 line-side and 14,600 point-side (693,500; 14,400 in the 693,100 graph). A line-side hub has one owner (p, i), p ∈ F, at each level (5 edges), a
point-side hub one owner (φ(p), i) per level, and each hub has 7 hub-graph edges. Hub graph: the Haar graph
(g,A) ~ (s g,B), s ∈ S, of Γ = (Z73⋊Z8)×Z5² (or PSL(2,9)×Z40 for 693,100). At level i the right cosets of an order-20 subgroup H_i (side A) or K_i
(side B) sit on the 10 points of one spread line (or the 10 lines through one ovoid point), 2 hubs per point.

## 2. Why it works

- **Two routes, not one.** For a freed point p and a line y at distance 5 from p with geodesic p–L1–u2–L3–u4–y, the
  targets behind y at levels ±1, ±2 are reached by a hop at u2 (2 + 1 + 3 steps) or at u4 (4 + 1 + 1). The earlier
  rule "freed points pairwise non-collinear" protects the first route only. It is enough that u2 and u4 are not both
  freed, i.e. F contains no collinear path p ~ u2 ~ u4 on two different lines. So F is a union of segments of pairwise
  opposite lines. Every point of F then blocks at least q + 1/(q+1) lines, so |F| ≤ 66,430·10/91 = 7,300, and a spread
  attains it. Check: with all 7,300 spread points freed and no hubs, the main graph has diameter 7 (orbcat, 0 pairs at
  distance > 7). One more freed point (point 0) breaks 82,720 ordered pairs.
- **Main-main pairs never need hubs** (previous item), so a hub may take its owners from different freed points at
  different levels. One owner per level is needed and enough: through (p_i, i) a line-side hub reaches every point
  within 7 and every line of level i within 6 (hexagon distance ≤ 5).
- **Opposite-side hubs are always within 7**: 1 + d_H(p, φ(p′)) + 1 ≤ 7 through any level.
- **Same-side hubs** must be within 6. Hubs of one cluster are within 4 through the main graph (p–L–p′, or φ(p)–φ(L)–φ(p′)),
  within 2 if they share an owner. These relations and the Haar edges are all left multiplications, so the hub distance
  is right-invariant: (g,A),(h,A) are within 6 if h g⁻¹ is within weighted distance 6 of e in a Cayley-type graph on
  Γ×{A,B} (Haar steps weight 1, cluster steps weight 4, co-owner steps weight 2). clhaar.c / clhaarP.c anneal S and
  the subgroups until every element is covered (cost 0). This is a sufficient condition; the main graph adds more routes.
- The subgroups must "mix": in SL(2,11)×Z11 every order-20 subgroup has trivial Z11 part, and the search stalls at cost
  88. In PSL(2,9)×Z40 (subgroups like Z5×Z4 with the Z4 in Z40) it reaches 0 in seconds.

### 5.3 Lifts of polygon covers are impossible for q ≥ 3 (sketch)

Take the record's shape H(q) × Z₅ + σ₁, σ₂ and any voltages in a group Γ ≠ 1 on all edges. Let u be a point in copy c and
v a point in copy c+1 such that the line ℓ = σ₁⁻¹(v) is at hexagon distance 5 from u (most pairs). A walk of length ≤ 6
from (u,c) to (v,c+1) must use σ₁ exactly once; before the hop it walks in copy c from u to some point x, after it from
σ₁(x) to v. With σ₁ a duality, the segment after the hop mirrors a walk from x to ℓ, so d(u,x) + d(x,ℓ) ≤ 5 = d(u,ℓ):
both segments are geodesics and x is one of the three points x₀ = u, x₂, x₄ of the unique hexagon geodesic from u to ℓ.
Walks with two or three hops (shift +2−1, +2+2+2, …) leave ≤ 4 or ≤ 3 hexagon steps and reach only targets in a few
twisted balls around u (at most about 38 % of the copy at q = 9). A geodesic 4-path x₀x₂x₄ has q extensions to a line
ℓ at distance 5, so all but a tiny fraction (about 0.38⁹) of 4-paths have a generic extension, which is all the counting
below needs. A generic pair is joined by exactly 3 walks. (With random matchings a pair has 1 + Poisson(λ ≈ 2.2) walks:
the hop at u, plus a hop at a point x at distance 2 or 4 whose matched line happens to lie close to v. About 11 % of
pairs then have a single walk, which rules out every lift at once. A matching design giving every pair ≥ 2 walks would
be a covering with λ ≈ 2.2 and no misses, from every source.) For Γ = Z₂ the three voltages are not all equal iff a
2-colouring of the points of each line (determined by the voltages) separates x₀ from x₂ on the line x₀x₂ or x₂ from x₄ on
the line x₂x₄. Call a point non-unique on a line if another point of the line has its colour. The condition says every
point is non-unique on at most one of its lines. But a 2-colouring of q+1 ≥ 3 points leaves at most one point alone in its
colour, so every line has at least q non-unique points: q·#lines ≤ #points, false since #lines = #points. The argument
needs every geodesic 4-path x₀x₂x₄ to start a generic pair; that holds for q ≥ 3 (most targets are generic), while at
q = 2 the two-hop routes reach about half of the targets and the hypothesis can fail. For Z₃ the three voltages must be
distinct, which forces the colour differences to be constant along paths and then equal to their own negatives, a
contradiction. A larger Γ needs even more walks. (Squad card r1-c2 tests this numerically at q = 2, 3.)

#### 5.3.1 Sheet constructions over a 3-copy cover (also negative, by counting)

Two ways to get 6·|H(9)| = 797,160 vertices at degree 12 from the 3-copy cover:
- **Z₂-lift of H(9) × Z₃ with two matchings of the same shift** (degree 10 + 2). A cross-copy pair now has 6 routes
  instead of 3, so the lemma above does not apply directly. But a pair of opposite points in one copy (hexagon distance 6)
  can only change sheet along its q+1 = 10 geodesics (two or three hops leave ≤ 4 or ≤ 3 hexagon steps). So the Z₂ voltage
  on each hexagon copy must be non-constant on the 10 geodesics of every opposite pair. That is ≈ 3.9·10⁹ conditions
  (point pairs and line pairs), each failing with probability 2/2¹⁰ for a random cochain, on 531,441 = 9⁶ free voltages.
  First moment: 2^531,441 · e^(−7.7·10⁶) ≪ 1, so only a very special algebraic cochain could work, and none is known.
  Card r1-c1 tests the small cases.
- **Doubling the 3-copy cover (degree 11) with one "full" (K₂,₂) edge per vertex.** The full edges form a perfect matching
  F of the base and are the only free sheet changes. A pair of collinear points has one walk of length 2 through their line L
  and, within the copy, no other walk of length ≤ 6. It needs an F-edge on that walk unless hops help, and F contains
  only 1 of the 10 edges at L. Most such pairs fail.

### 5.5 Best result: 664,304 (table 664,300)

**A bipartite graph of maximum degree 12 and diameter 7 on 664,304 vertices**, 4 more than the table. Files:
`research/squads/d12D7/graphs/` (edge list `d12D7_n664304.edges.gz`, matchings `d12D7_n664304.match.gz`, builder
`build_d12D7_n664304.py`, check `d12D7_n664304_check.txt`).

Construction ("gadget on a polygon cover"). Start from the table's graph: H(9) × Z₅ with two matchings σ₁, σ₂
(points → lines), voltages 1 and 2. Pick a point X. Delete the 10 hop edges at X ((X,c) ~ (σ_t(X), c+t), all c, t).
Add two new line-side vertices W₁, W₂ joined to (X,c) for every c, and two new point-side vertices W₁′, W₂′, with W_t′
joined to (σ_t(X), c) for every c. Degrees: X and σ_t(X) regain 12, the new vertices have degree 5.

Why it works. The cover theorem's route for a pair hops at one end: at the point end, at the line end of a line–line pair,
or at one of the 10 points of the line end. So after the deletion only pairs with an endpoint in
{(X,c), (σ₁(X),c), (σ₂(X),c)} can lose their route. The new vertices reach every copy through their 5 neighbours
(1 + 6 and 1 + 5 steps). What remains is a covering condition on the matchings near X and σ_t(X):
F1: every point outside Ball₄(X) is within distance 3 of σ_t(x) for some x at distance 2 from X, or lies on σ_t(x) for some
x at distance 4 (route X → x → hop → v, length ≤ 6). F2: the dual condition for σ_t(X), using backward hops at lines
at distance 2 or 4. Random matchings miss 23,855 targets; `d12D7/opus/gadget.c` repairs them by targeted swaps in 2 minutes.

Checks: BFS from the 19 special and new vertices (gadget.c); `symcheck.c` (only reads the edge list: simple, degree,
bipartite, verifies that the copy shift is an automorphism, then bit-parallel BFS from all 132,864 orbit
representatives): **RESULT VALID d=12 D=7 n=664304, diameter 7**; the Python builder rebuilds the same edge set from the
definition (sha256 2eb63199…2831). apsp.c (all pairs, no symmetry): see the check file.

Earlier claim corrected by squad card r1-c3. Section 2 originally said every edge of the polygon cover is critical
("every edge lies on the unique short route of ~5q⁴ pairs"). That is false. A generic pair has 3 routes hopping at
