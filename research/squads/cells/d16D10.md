## Why the cell was open, and why it falls

For a Haar graph H(G,S) with |S| = 16 and D = 10 the binding condition is that every element of G is reached from
(e,A) by an odd word of length ≤ 9: W = 16·(1 + 15² + 15⁴ + 15⁶ + 15⁸) = 41,189,313,616 words. At the table order
(N = |G| = 2,059,753,666) λ = W/N = 20.0 and μ = N·e^(−λ) = 4.3, far below what the orbit search of
research/structured solves at D = 10. The cell was open only because |G| > 2³¹ is beyond the existing tools:
haar_gen3 / haar_sym use 32-bit indices and ~14–18 bytes per element (~31 GB here), on a 15 GB box.

## The tool: research/bighaar/haar_big.c

G = SL(2,p) × Z_n or PSL(2,p) × Z_n, p prime, gcd(p, n) = 1, 64-bit indices, even D.
- Rows are the left cosets of C = U × Z_n (U the upper unitriangular group), which is cyclic of order L = p·n. A
  generator maps a row to a row with a rotation, so one BFS level is one window read per (row, generator). Rows are
  stored doubled (2L bits) so every rotation is a single contiguous window. Memory N/2 bytes: 1.2 GB at N = 2.2·10⁹.
- Sparse levels push from an element list (the last push level with prefetching and no list); dense levels pull
  (16 windows ORed per 512-bit chunk in registers, rows streamed out with non-temporal stores); the last level only
  counts zeros, stops each chunk when it is full, and lists the uncovered elements for the targeted moves.
- Search: haar_sym's orbit mode (S = {e} ∪ m α-orbits of size k, α = conjugation × (z ↦ rz)), best-of-K with the
  exact abort, targeted moves, schedules counted in steps.
- Tested against an independent brute-force BFS (research/bighaar/tests/brute_cost.py) on ~300 random sets (SL and
  PSL, n = 1 … 211, D = 6, 8; all push/pull paths, 1–3 threads): every cost identical. Known records (10,10),
  (8,10), (12,10), (7,8) evaluate to 0.
- One evaluation at N = 2.3·10⁹: ~3.5 s on one core with 4 such processes running (memory-bandwidth bound: levels
  6, 7, 8 cost ~0.9 s each, level 9 ~0.5 s; the first evaluation of a process also pays ~10 s of page faults).
- Added on 2026-10-25:
  - **FIXF=1** (D = 10): before a candidate is evaluated, its holes among the α-fixed elements are counted exactly by
    meet in the middle (x is within 9 of (e,A) iff v·x ∈ Y_5 for some v = s6 s7⁻¹ s8 s9⁻¹; one v per α-orbit suffices
    for fixed x). ~0.1–0.2 s per candidate. Only candidates with no fixed hole (FIXMAX = 0) are evaluated, up to
    FIXTRY = 200 tries per candidate. Agrees with the full evaluation on every α-fixed element of 55 test sets (48
    small random sets with D = 10, k = 3, 5, and 7 at N = 2.3·10⁹).
  - **ZQ**: the Z_n-uniformity score of S (non-backtracking walks of odd length ≤ 9 counted per residue of Z_n, holes
    predicted as Σ_z |Q|·exp(−λ A_z / mean A) relative to μ). ZQONLY=1 with RANDORB or EVAL prints it, no BFS.
  - **BOUNDCUR=1**: in the greedy phase every candidate aborts as soon as it is worse than the current S.
  - UNCPRINT=1 with EVAL lists the holes; FIX2=1 a variant of the screen (probes X_4 instead of building Y_5); the
    screen now stops at the first fixed hole; LEVPOP=1 counts the elements of the pulled levels.
- Bug fixed at the end of the session: last_level counted holes in an int, so a candidate with more than 2³¹ holes
  (one that covers almost nothing) overflowed it and wrote out of bounds. That was squad c8's segfault, reproduced
  under gdb at the same seed and step. It only ever crashed a run; it cannot report a false cost 0, and every find
  above was checked by independent checkers.
- Checkers for these sizes: research/frontier/haarcheck_sl.c (SL, ~33 min at N = 2.2e9), research/structured/tools/
  haarcheck_psl.c (PSL, ~52–60 min), research/squads/d16D10-r1/c5/hcheck5.c (SL, OpenMP) and the new
  research/squads/d16D10-r2/checker/hcheckpsl.c (PSL, written by a Haiku card from the definitions, ~6 min on one
  core; 27/27 agreement with haarcheck_psl on test files, and it agrees on every (16,10) find below).

## What we learned on 2026-10-25

1. **With k = 5 the last holes are α-fixed.** α fixes exactly (torus containing g) × {z = 0}: ~p elements in SL(2,p),
   ~p/2 in PSL(2,p). The words reaching a fixed element come in α-orbits of 5, so it is uncovered with probability
   ≈ e^(−λ/5) ≈ 0.028 instead of e^(−λ) — and a fixed hole costs 1, not 5. SL(2,151) × Z661, k = 5: cost 2 after 41
   evaluations, then stuck for 170; both holes were α-fixed (they commute with g, z = 0). Random S there average 5.3
   fixed holes. Hence FIXF: screen the ~75–150 fixed elements first, then pay for a full evaluation.
2. **k = 5 beats k = 3** at μ ≈ 31: free hole orbits ≈ μ/k, and the k = 5 fixed holes are cheap to screen. k = 3 at
   PSL(2,61) × Z20287 (μ 38.9, squad c5): best 45. k = 15 (m = 1) is hopeless: e^(−λ/15) ≈ 0.3 per fixed element,
   and the groups with few fixed elements have huge n (squad c8, PSL(2,31) × Z154591: cost 178,725; its ZQ score is
   1,614, so the Z_n non-uniformity alone explains the count).
3. **The search is close to random sampling with a little memory.** The mean cost of the best-of-4 candidates does not
   fall during a run (early/mid/late means 24/27/28 at SL(2,151) × Z661), but candidates around a good S average ~6
   free hole orbits against ~7.3 for random S. Once T reaches TMIN the run is a greedy wait for a lucky candidate:
   at μ 31 about 1 % of steps (the first two finds took 282 and 193 evaluations).
4. **Group shape: large p, small n.** Random-sample screen at μ ≈ 31 (k = 5, 10 samples each, research/bighaar/runs/
   screen/): free hole orbits per S were 7.3 (PSL(2,151) × Z1321), 7.2 (SL(2,151) × Z661), 7.2 (PSL(2,311) × Z151),
   6.8 (PSL(2,421) × Z61), 7.9 (PSL(2,101) × Z4411), 14.5 (SL(2,71) × Z6361), 59.7 (PSL(2,61) × Z20071); μ/5 ≈ 6.2.
   Fixed holes grow with p: 2.3 (PSL 151), 5.3 (SL 151), 5.7 (PSL 311), 6.0 (PSL 421). PSL(2,~150–190) × Z_(~660–1330)
   is the sweet spot: few free orbits and a cheap fixed screen (e^2.3 ≈ 10 tries per candidate).
5. **Why large n hurts:** the Z_n part of a word depends only on its signed letter counts, so the walks are unevenly
   spread over the residues of Z_n. The ZQ score orders the groups the same way (median 1.000 for the p = 151
   groups, 1.02, 1.05, 1.37 for n = 4411, 6361, 20071, 23 for PSL(2,31) × Z154571, whose squad run ended at cost 355).
   It does not explain everything: at ZQ ≈ 1.0, SL(2,71) × Z6361 still averages ~9.5 free orbits.
6. **Effort per find grew slowly with μ.** Evaluations spent per rung, all runs together: μ 30.9: 511 (1 find),
   35.7: 193 (1), 39.3: ~2,570 (1), 43.6: ~1,455 (1), 47.3: ~1,800 (0; abandoned when μ 52.1 solved), 52.1: 877 (1),
   59.4: ~4,100 (1), 62.8: 1,024 (0), 66.1: 1,502 (0, best 15), 68.6: 1,273 (0); the last three were stopped. At
   ~0.33 evaluations per second per core (4 processes share the memory bandwidth) that is 0.2–3.5 core-hours per find.
   The model P(cost 0) ≈ e^(−μ/5) per candidate would have predicted ~70× more effort at μ 52 than at μ 31 and ~300×
   more at μ 59; we saw ~2× and ~8×.
7. **No cheap predictor of a candidate's cost.** The sizes of BFS levels 7 and 8 correlate only −0.25 with the final cost
   over 24 random S (the spread of |X_8| moves the expected holes by ~4 %), so there is no early abort before level 9;
   the cost of a candidate is essentially Poisson noise around a group-level mean. What helps: the FIXF screen, small
   n, k = 5, BOUNDCUR, and restarting greedy (TMAX = TMIN) from the best set of a stalled run with a new seed.

## What failed and why

- SL(2,487) × Z19 (μ 15.5): stuck at cost 6 after 41 evaluations; 2× slower per evaluation (237K short rows).
- k = 3 at μ ≥ 31 (SL(2,151) × Z661: stuck at 10; × Z673: stuck at 21): μ/3 free orbits.
- Small p / huge n (PSL(2,61) × Z20071, PSL(2,31) × Z154571, k = 15 on PSL(2,31) × Z154591): Z_n non-uniformity.
- TMIN 0.5 instead of 0.15 (squad c6): no gain; candidates are 2–10 orbits worse than the current S, so a warmer
  floor accepts none of them either. Three 200 s restarts instead of one 600 s run (squad c7): worse.
- FIX2 (screen against X_4 instead of building Y_5): only 1.2× faster; the fixed-element probes dominate. Stopping the
  screen at the first fixed hole (FIXMAX + 1) gave the real gain (~2.5× less screening).
- Not implemented, and why: an α-quotient BFS (store one row per α-orbit) needs rows on which α acts trivially, i.e.
  cosets of the torus (75 bits), and such short rows cost more memory traffic than they save; k = 7 with one fixed
  generator has ~0.1 fixed holes per fixed element, so either p is small (huge n) or the screen needs ~e^8 tries.
