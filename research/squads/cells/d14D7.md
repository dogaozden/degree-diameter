## Best result (second session, 2026-10-24 afternoon)

**1,771,870 vertices** (table 1,771,560, +310): the DGQ cover of H(11) (2 matchings over Z5) with 155 "type-2 units"
(75 on matching 1, 80 on matching 2) and a random hub graph. Checked by `uhcheck` (all 354,312 base sources and the 310
new vertices; the copy shift is an automorphism fixing the new vertices): 0 pairs beyond 7. Independent `apsp_cyc` run
on the edge list started (research/cell147/units/sols/). A 158-unit version (79 flags on both matchings, 1,771,876)
passes the unit-endpoint check; the final number depends on the packing (below).

The type-2 unit: for an incident flag p ∈ l = sigma_t(p), let W = the 121 points collinear with p off l and K = the
121 lines meeting l off p; if sigma_t maps W onto K, both endpoints of the unit reach everything within 7 (routes of
length 6 through the matching edge at a point of W; proof in research/cell147/units/README.md and t2design.c). So a unit
costs 121 assignments instead of 14,641. Units of one matching need disjoint zones ({p} ∪ W, {l} ∪ K): freed points
pairwise opposite, or at distance 4 with the path leaving one of them along its flag line, and dually for lines. This
is a packing problem: random packings jam at ~75-80 per matching (cap 1,452).

## Earlier best (first session)

**1,771,562 vertices** (one unit; checked) and **1,771,564** (two units, one per matching; see the status line in
research/cell147/units/README.md). Construction: the table graph itself, i.e. the Delorme–Gómez–Quisquater polygon
cover (incidence graph of the split Cayley hexagon H(11), plus 2 point→line perfect matchings, lifted by Z5 with
polygon voltages 0 and matching t voltage t), with "units". A unit deletes one matching edge (p, l) in all 5 copies
and adds two new vertices z ~ {(p,c)}, z' ~ {(l,c)} (degree 5). The matchings are not random: they are prescribed on
the points at distance 4 from p (and the lines at distance 4 from l) so that every point opposite p gets a route
p –4– x –hop– σ(x) –1– y of length 6 (the 14,641 lines at distance 4 from a line M ∋ p, not projecting onto p,
partition the 161,051 points opposite p). Proof sketch, tools and rebuild commands: research/cell147/units/README.md.

Checks: `ucheck12` (written here from the definitions; uses only the cyclic shift of copies, which fixes z and z')
over all 354,312 base sources and the new vertices: 0 pairs beyond distance 7. The checker agrees with apsp on every
small case (valid and invalid). An apsp run on the 1,771,562 edge list (183 MB, sha256 5375337d…fd97f30) was started.

The same construction beats every Delorme–Gómez–Quisquater value, D = 5 (GQ) and D = 7 (GH), by 2j vertices

## What was tried and what failed (numbers)

- **Haar / Cayley graphs.** The size law (research/frontier/SIZE_LAW.md) puts random-like Haar graphs near
  1.0–1.07M here; the table is 1.38× its first-moment ceiling. Orbit search does not move that ceiling. LPS-type
  Ramanujan Cayley graphs of PGL(2,q) with 14 quaternion generators: diameter 7 only up to q = 59 (205,320 vertices;
  card c16). As expected, best expanders are worse than random-like here.
- **One more copy than the polygon cover (m = 2j+2).** Works at small q: GH(2)+1 matching over Z4 (504 vertices,
  apsp VALID) and GH(3)+2 over Z6 (4,368, apsp VALID). These are not records. The extra element (3 in Z6 with hop
  voltages {1,4}) is reached only by two-hop walks, whose total capacity is about 1.75 |P| at q = 11. Random matchings
  leave 15.6 % of copy 3 uncovered (27,600 misses per source). A Z2 twist on polygon edges gives 13,000, and random
  polygon voltages 8,000 (card c9). GH(5)+2 over Z6 (would be (8,7) 46,872): annealing only 5,032 → 4,109 (sampled),
  and card c10 got −4 % on the full cost. Other (14,7) candidates with random matchings: GH(9)+4 over Z14 6,031
  misses/source, GH(8)+5 over Z24 5,290, GH(7)+6 over Z46 5,794 (cards c3 and Opus). Counting (card c12) does not
  exclude m = 6..8 at q = 11. It is far beyond local search, though: an algebraic choice of matchings would be needed,
  and none was found (geometric matchings such as dualities make the two-hop route terms coincide).
- **Buildings** (Weyl-distance graphs, card c1): nothing with D ≤ 7 above 1.77M in types A2–A5 (others not scanned;
  a pruning bug in the scanner was fixed).
- **Lifts of GQ(q) or GH(q) without extra edges:** impossible below D = g − 1 (girth argument, card c8 and the
  voltage-lifts session).
- **Route-chart lifts** (degree = letters × scalars): 2 × F7 on a cycle controller reaches at most 1,647,086.
- **Polygon cover + extra vertices** (card c14, annealing at q = 3): +60 on GH(3)+2 over Z5. This is the idea
  behind the units.

## Limits and next steps

- Units add 2 vertices per deleted matching-edge orbit. With exact designs, units on different matchings never
  conflict. Units on the same matching compete for prescribed assignments: about 29k of the 177k assignments per
  unit, so at most about 6 per matching. The new vertices also need the unit points (and the unit lines) to be
  pairwise within hexagon distance 4. A joint design (a class-level flow between constraint signatures) could fit
  several units per matching, giving roughly +10 to +30. A residual-only repair (rely on random routes, patch the
  ~4,450 misses) did not converge at q = 11, because swaps destroy other random coverage.
- A real jump (2,125,872 = one more copy) needs matchings with algebraic structure that tile the two-hop route
  regions. None is known.
