**Type-2 unit.** For an incident flag p ∈ l = sigma_t(p), let W be the 121 points collinear with p off l and K the 121
lines meeting l off p. If sigma_t maps W onto K, both endpoints of the unit reach everything within 7 (routes of
length 6; proof in research/cell147/units/README.md). So a unit costs 121 prescribed assignments instead of 14,641.
Units of one matching need disjoint zones ({p} ∪ W and {l} ∪ K). Overlapping zones are never compatible: that would
need a 10-cycle in the hexagon.

**Packing = partial ovoid.** Points of H(q) are at distance ≤ 4 iff they are orthogonal on the quadric Q(6,q). In an
elliptic hyperplane section E (X4 = -X0 at q = 11) the hexagon lines form a Hermitian spread: 1,332 pairwise opposite
lines partitioning E. Flags (p, spread line through p) with pairwise non-orthogonal points p never conflict. Units per
matching = size of a partial ovoid of the elliptic quadric Q^-(5,11) = E:

| method | points |
|---|---|
| random packings of flags (any lines) | 75–91 |
| local search on E's orthogonality graph | 155–169 |
| orbits of an order-12 rotation of the anisotropic plane A of E = A ⊥ V4 | 216 |
| orbits of an order-4 isometry (squad card r3c3) | **240** |

The order-12 rotation's orbits correspond to points b of PG(3,11) off the hyperbolic quadric of V4. Two orbits are
compatible iff the line bb' is a secant. The maximum is 17 orbits, proven by exhaustive branch and bound on a
vertex-transitive graph; adding 12 fixed points gives 216, which is maximal.

**What did not work.** Super-hubs (no cover of all points by ≤ Δ hubs); symmetric packings from monomial automorphisms
or Singer cycles of SL(3,11) (no usable orbits); combining several elliptic sections (no gain); the older type-4 designs
(5 units per matching).

## What was tried and what failed (numbers)

- **Haar / Cayley graphs.** The size law (research/frontier/SIZE_LAW.md) puts random-like Haar graphs near
  1.0–1.07M here; the table is 1.38× its first-moment ceiling. Orbit search does not move that ceiling. LPS-type
  Ramanujan Cayley graphs of PGL(2,q) with 14 quaternion generators: diameter 7 only up to q = 59 (205,320 vertices;
  card c16). As expected, best expanders are worse than random-like here.
- **One more copy than the polygon cover (m = 2j+2).** Works at small q: GH(2)+1 matching over Z4 (504 vertices,
  apsp VALID) and GH(3)+2 over Z6 (4,368, apsp VALID). These are not records. The extra element (3 in Z6 with hop
  voltages {1,4}) is reached only by two-hop walks, whose total capacity is about 1.75 |P| at q = 11. Random matchings
  leave 15.6 % of copy 3 uncovered (27,600 misses per source). A Z2 twist on polygon edges gives 13,000, and random
  polygon voltages 8,000 (card c9). GH(5)+2 over Z6 (would be (8,7) 46,872): annealing only 5,032 → 4,109 (sampled),
  and card c10 got −4 % on the full cost. Other (14,7) candidates with random matchings: GH(9)+4 over Z14 6,031
  misses/source, GH(8)+5 over Z24 5,290, GH(7)+6 over Z46 5,794 (cards c3 and Opus). Counting (card c12) does not
  exclude m = 6..8 at q = 11. It is far beyond local search, though: an algebraic choice of matchings would be needed,
  and none was found (geometric matchings such as dualities make the two-hop route terms coincide).
- **Buildings** (Weyl-distance graphs, card c1): nothing with D ≤ 7 above 1.77M in types A2–A5 (others not scanned;
  a pruning bug in the scanner was fixed).
- **Lifts of GQ(q) or GH(q) without extra edges:** impossible below D = g − 1 (girth argument, card c8 and the
  voltage-lifts session).
- **Route-chart lifts** (degree = letters × scalars): 2 × F7 on a cycle controller reaches at most 1,647,086.
- **Polygon cover + extra vertices** (card c14, annealing at q = 3): +60 on GH(3)+2 over Z5. This is the idea
  behind the units.

## Limits and next steps

- **The bottleneck is the packing.** n = 1,771,560 + 4N, where N is the size of a partial ovoid of the elliptic quadric
  Q^-(5,11) = E (pairwise non-orthogonal points; each gives one type-2 unit per matching, and the two matchings can use
  the same set). The trivial bound is q^3 + 1 = 1,332, so the gap is large, but every method here stops at 240 =
  2(q^2 - 1): local search (155–221), annealing (193), LNS with exact refills around 240 (rigid: every outside point is
  orthogonal to at least 8 set points), and orbit unions of about 100 isometry classes of orders 4–660, including
  order 11k (unipotent part, at most 221), Singer-type order 37/111 (no usable orbits) and two-generator groups.
- **What the 240 set is.** It is a union of 60 orbits of g = 1_A ⊕ J, with E = A ⊥ V4 (A = (x0, x3) anisotropic,
  V4 = (x1, x2, x5, x6) hyperbolic) and J a complex structure on V4 (J^2 = -1; on V4 = M2(F_q) with the det form,
  J = left multiplication by [[0, 1], [-1, 0]]); a J-invariant 240 set found here uses 6 of the 12 directions of A
  (56, 56, 32, 32, 32, 32 points). A cleaner description: the points of E with N(a) a square form the group G = (T × SL(2,q))/±1 (T = norm-1
  elements of A = F_{q^2}), and two of them conflict iff their quotient (u, Y) has tr Y = u + 1/u. So a subgroup K of
  SL(2,q) with no unipotent element gives |K| pairwise non-orthogonal points (one coset). At q = 11, K = 2.A5 (the binary
  icosahedral group, order 120 = q^2 - 1, the exceptional nearfield case) has 132 right orbits on E, and exactly two
  are compatible: 2 × 120 = 240 (uni11/grpperm.py, orbitclique_q). The same count, q^2 - 1 = |2.A4|, explains 48 at
  q = 5. At q = 7 the order-48 group 2.S4 fits only one coset (48), and q = 9, 13 have no such subgroup. So 2(q^2 - 1)
  at q = 5 and 11 comes from Galois's exceptional cases (subgroups of index q in SL(2,q)), not from a general
  construction. q = 8 also reaches 2(q^2 - 1) = 126 (GF(8) spread ILS; structure not identified). At q = 7 a plain
  local search later found 98 > 2(q^2 - 1) = 96 (4 of 57 short runs; the others give 82–96), so 2(q^2 - 1) is not a
  ceiling there. The 98 set has 8 "centers" (points of E collinear with q^2 = 49 set points each) and a stabilizer of
  order 336. In the model V = Sym^4(F_q^2) ⊥ <n0> (binary quartics with the invariant form; E elliptic for a suitable
  Q(n0)), the centers are the rational normal curve, and the set is the union of a 14-orbit and an 84-orbit of
  PSL(2,7) × <reflection in n0^⊥> (uni11/psl2.py reproduces 98 exactly). The same model at q = 11 gives nothing
  (PSL(2,11): only the 12-point curve is usable; 2.A5 < SL(2,11) acting on quartics: 240 again, as 60 + 6 × 30). At
  q = 5 it gives 30. At q = 9 and 13 the best values are below 2(q^2 - 1) (150, 294).
  A construction that gives 2(q^2 - 1) for every q, or a proof that N ≤ 2(q^2 - 1) for this structure, is the open
  question. Each extra point gives +4 vertices at (14,7), +2 at (13,7) and +6 at (15,7).
- Other directions that did not help: super-hubs (no cover of all points by ≤ Δ hubs), several elliptic sections at
  once, flags off E (random packings of general flags reach only 75–91), and one more copy than the polygon cover (see
  above).
- A real jump (2,125,872 = one more copy) would still need matchings with algebraic structure that tile the two-hop
  route regions. None is known.
