## Best result

**7,032** = 12 · 586 (round 3, research/d11D5/graphs/d11D5_n7032_CHECK.md, apsp VALID, diameter 5). Base: W(8) with one
9-flag switch (a new point x* on 9 pairwise disjoint old lines, each giving up one point to a new line l*), a
random-like sigma, Z6 voltages 1 and 4 on the doubled matching and 3·nu on the flags. How it was found:
1. r2 annealing from sigma = delta: structural cost 2 at best (many runs).
2. r3 squad card c2's clause-weighting search (wpo): structural cost 0 in 325 CPU s. Every other local method stopped at 2.
3. nu: nujoint annealing (34 left), greedy sigma swaps that give violated pairs a sigma route (2 left), annealing with
   those swaps as moves (0).
The family's counting ceiling is 14,796 (n ≤ 1,233), so each further pair is +12. The same one-pair pipeline gave the
side results (10,5) 4,812 (W(7)) and (12,5) 9,852 (W(9)) (research/d11D5/graphs/). Next pairs (n = 587) were being
tried at the end of round 3; see research/squads/handoff/resume3-cell-11-5.md.

## Why the other methods won't do it

- Lifts of W(q) + parallel jumps: the reachable sheets are 2(2p+1) unless the geodesics cover Z₃, and they don't
  (W(3) UNSAT, every search far from 0). 7,020 is the maximum of that family at Δ = 11.
- Chart lifts need Δ = k·q with k ≥ 3; 11 is prime, and mixed letter/unit versions stop at 2,916.
- Haar/Cayley graphs: the size law gives about 5,300 at (11,5).
