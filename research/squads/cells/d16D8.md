## Result

**No record.** The table graph is 45·|I(H(13))| = 45 · 804,468 (Delorme, Gómez, Quisquater, "On large bipartite
graphs", never published; the wiki lists it as "submitted", and no description of the construction was found online).
Everything we can build is far below it. The closest is extrapolated: symmetric repair designs on H(13) would give
roughly 24-33 copies (18.5-26M vertices) by the counting model below. Round 3 (symmetric repair on the hexagon itself)
is summarised in §4.

## 1. The family and why it is hard

DGQ's D = 8 values for Δ = q+3 are m = 5·⌊(q+5)/2⌋ copies of I(H(q)): (10,8) = 30·|H(7)|, (11,8) = 30·|H(8)|,
(12,8) = 35·|H(9)|, (14,8) = 40·|H(11)|, (16,8) = 45·|H(13)|, and (13,8) = 15·|H(11)| with one matching (Δ = q+2).
Our model throughout: a Z_m-lift of I + 2 perfect matchings (points -> lines), vertex (x,g); diameter 8.
Literature (Oct 25 web searches): the DGQ paper is listed as "submitted" on the wiki; Delorme's 1985 JGT paper and the
Pineda-Villavicencio et al. diameter-6 compound papers do not describe a diameter-8 construction. Nothing found.

**Counting model (fits all our data).** Let W = (q+3)(q+2)^6 / N be the average number of walks of length 7 from a
point to a given line of the base (the binding budget: opposite-side pairs must be within 7), S the number of source
orbits (2N with no symmetry, 2N/|C| with a semiregular collineation group C), and N·m the number of (line, sheet)
targets. A random-like design needs W/m ≳ ln(S·N·m)/c for zero misses, i.e. m ≈ c·W / ln(S·N·m). Values of c
(computed in this session from the squad results):

| case | W | S | m | c |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| W(13) testbed, random | 340 | 4,760 | 14 | 0.78 |
| W(13), targeted repair (r2 c7) | 340 | 4,760 | 23 | 1.31 |
| W(13), C_85-invariant anneal (r2 c4) | 340 | 56 | 28 | 1.24 |
| W(7), repair (r2 c7) | 164 | 800 | 18 | 1.71 |
| H(3), round 1 focus search | 258 | 728 | 28 | 1.72 |
| H(5), random | 241 | 7,812 | 13 | 1.07 |
| **DGQ** H(5) / H(7) / H(13) | 241 / 271 / 453 | all | 25 / 30 / 45 | **2.12 / 2.64 / 3.01** |

Random-like designs have c ≤ 1.7 and their c falls as q grows; DGQ's structured graphs have c rising with q. For H(13)
the largest semiregular cyclic group we know has order 157 (the torus of order q²−q+1, a prime, so it is semiregular;
not built here). The q²+q+1 = 183 torus of SL(3,13) contains the scalars of order 3 on V_3, which fix points, so only
its order-61 subgroup is semiregular (built: hexsym.py, 13,188 source orbits). With S = 5,124, c = 1.3-1.8 gives
m ≈ 24-33. Beating 45 needs c > 3, so the cell needs a derandomized construction, not a better search.

## 2. What was tried (all negative for the cell)

Round 1 (H(3), H(5); research/squads/d16D8-r1, LOG.md): random, annealed, X(M)-skeleton, incident matchings, jump
distance, edge-colouring and coordinate flag shifts, group choice, targeted repair. Best VALID m: 28 at q=3 (DGQ 20),
13-16 at q=5 (DGQ 25).

Round 2 (W(q) testbed: Z_m-lifts of I(W(q)) + 2 matchings at diameter 6 = 4 + 2, same degree and walk scaling, full
check ~1-3 s at q = 13; research/squads/d16D8-r2): plain annealing 14 at q=13; targeted repair 23; C_85-invariant designs
28 (both verified VALID by Opus, structure checked with research/d16D8/checkgq.py); every level skeleton Z_L x Z_k with
k ≥ 2 sheets is worse than unstructured lifts; algebraic flag voltages (PG(1,q) parameters, Singer indices, discrete
logs) behave like potentials and lose; the "rainbow" hypothesis (q+3 = 2(k−1)) is refuted.

Opus side experiments (research/d16D8/):
- Typed levels (copies of H and its dual with identity maps on friendly transitions, the idea that won (16,7)): worse
  than untyped under both random voltages and annealing (q=3, m=25: 1,026 vs 118 uncovered).
- Full-coverage compound designs (zero flag voltages; every vertex sees all m sheets among its 2q+6 cost-≤2 images,
  a sufficient local condition, so m ≤ 2q+7): localdes.c reaches local cost 0 only at m = 12 (q=7) and ~16 (q=13).
- Geodesic-coverage covers: Z_k flag voltages such that the q+1 geodesics between every opposite pair carry all k
  values. geocov.c plateaus (q=5, k=3: 14% of opposite pairs miss a value after 6·10^5 moves at any temperature), so
  such covers apparently do not exist even at k = (q+1)/2.
- Route-chart lifts (the (16,5) method) need a controller of 1,105+ vertices per side with degree 4 and all opposite
  pairs joined by non-singular non-backtracking walks of length exactly 7 (fibre F_4^7), against 420 per side in the
  (4,7) record: impossible. Kronecker products of bipartite graphs and vertex duplication give < 20M.

## 3. Structural notes for whoever finds the construction

- Going from diameter n+1 to n+2 (n = 6) adds budget only for same-side pairs: opposite-side pairs must still be
  within n+1 = 7. In a 5-level skeleton the only routes to a line of level +1 or +2 are the q+1 geodesics from the hop
  image, so k ≤ (q+1) + O(1) sheets per level. DGQ's k = (q+5)/2 = (q+1)/2 + 2 fits "geodesic voltages come in pairs
  plus two extra routes"; for one matching their (13,8) count 15 = 3·(q−1)/2 at q = 11 ≡ 3 (mod 4) fits a sheet group
  F_q^*/{±1}. These are numerological hints only.
- With zero flag voltages, the "full images" at cost ≤ 2 number 2q+6 per vertex (own hops 2, neighbours' hops 2(q+1),
  hop-hop 2), so m ≤ 2q+7 = 33 at q = 13 for designs that use only full images; DGQ's 45 needs partial (pencil-type)
  coverage at cost 3 for about 12 more sheets per vertex.
- The (n+2) polygon-cover theorem ({0} ∪ ±T ∪ (T−T) ∪ (T+T) = Z_M for any matchings, M ≤ 10) works through a hop at
  each END of the route: x -> M_i(x) -> geodesic -> M_j^{-1}(y) -> y.

## 4. Round 3 (symmetric repair on the hexagon itself)

Tools: hexsym.py (a semiregular C ≤ SL(3,q) ≤ G2(q) in hexgen coordinates: x -> (A x_012, x_3, A^-T x_456), A a
power of a Singer cycle; verified to map every line to a line; C_13 on H(3), C_31 on H(5), C_19 on H(7)), symbase.py
(random C-invariant designs, orbit sources), symrepair.c (targeted repair keeping C-invariance; orbit-source
evaluation is exact: 74 orbit-source misses x 31 = 2,294 all-source misses in a test).

Best VALID m (all checked by Opus with baselift from all sources and checkhex.py / checkgq.py; designs in
research/squads/d16D8-r3/best/):

| polygon | group | best m | c | DGQ count |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H(3) | C_13 | 31 | 1.61 | 20 |
| H(5) | C_31 | 20 (22 ends 10 orbit-pairs short) | 1.39 | 25 |
| H(7) | C_19 | 17 | 1.28 | 30 |
| W(11) | C_61 | 27 | 1.4 | 40 |
| W(13) | C_85 | 32 | 1.44 | 45 |

Equivariant matching moves, twisted invariance (psi(g.e) = u·psi(e)), zero flag voltages, M2 = g^K∘M1 and related
voltages on the two matchings were all no better or worse than plain symmetric repair. On hexagons the gap to DGQ
widens with q (+11, −5, −13 at q = 3, 5, 7), as the model predicts.

## 5. Verdict and what would change it

The cell will not fall to search in the lift family: every method we have sits at c ≈ 1.1-1.7 in the counting model,
DGQ's graphs are at c ≈ 3 for q = 13, and the gap grows with q. Extrapolated to H(13) with the best semiregular group,
symmetric repair would give about 24-33 copies (18.5-26.5M vertices) against 45 (36.2M). Two things could change this:

1. **DGQ's construction itself.** It is unpublished ("On large bipartite graphs", Delorme–Gómez–Quisquater,
   "submitted"). With it in hand, the (16,7) recipe (dual-typed copies, friendly identity transitions, surgery, hubs;
   research/squads/cells/d16D7.md) is the natural way to add vertices. A single extra hub pair would beat the table.
2. **A derandomized design** in which every pair's walk voltages cover all sheets by construction (as in the D = 7
   proof "for any matchings"). Section 3 lists the constraints such a design must meet. We found none: level
   skeletons, typed duals, coordinate voltages, colourings, full-coverage local designs and geodesic-coverage covers
   all lose to random.

Tools left for a successor: research/d16D8/baselift.c (any base, Z_k ≤ 64), symrepair.c, symbase.py, hexsym.py
(semiregular groups on H(q), q prime; H(13): C_61, file hex/sym_q13_r61.json is regenerated by `python3 hexsym.py 13 61`),
gq.py (W(q) testbed), checkhex.py / checkgq.py (structure checks).
