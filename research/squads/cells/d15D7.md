## What the table value is
2,480,184 = 7·|H(11)| = 7 · 354,312: the (2k+1)-copy construction (Delorme, Gómez, Quisquater; rebuilt by exp2-solo and
exp2-squad today) with q = 11, k = 3 (Δ = q + 1 + k). Copies c ∈ Z_7 of the incidence graph of H(11), cross edges
(p,c) ~ (φ_j(p), c+j), j = 1, 2, 3, for any bijections φ_j: points → lines. Every choice of bijections gives diameter 7.
Among copy designs this is the best at Δ = 15 (3·H(13) = 2,413,404, 11·H(9) = 1,461,460) and 2k+1 copies is the limit
of any copy design (exp2-solo §4.3).

## The construction: hubs on 7 copies
Take φ_1 = φ_2 = φ_3 = M. A *freed flag* (p, l) (p on l, M(p) = l) loses p's 21 cross edges; add 3 line-side hubs
adjacent to the 7 copies of p and 3 point-side hubs adjacent to the 7 copies of l. Degrees stay ≤ 15 (hubs: 7 + at
most 8 hub-graph edges). **n = 2,480,184 + 6K for K freed flags.** (exp2-squad's trick on 3 copies gives +2 per freed
pair; on 7 copies one bijection frees 3 slots at each end, so +6, with exactly the same conditions on M.)

**Lemma** (header of `research/d15D7/kcopy_hub.c`). In a bipartite graph, diameter ≤ 7 iff same-side distances are
≤ 6. A point pair in copies c, c+j (j = 1..3) is joined by (a,c) → (M(a),c+j) → hexagon (≤ 5) unless a is freed; a line
pair in copies c, c−j by (l,c) → (M⁻¹(l),c−j) → hexagon (≤ 5) unless M⁻¹(l) is freed; same-copy pairs by the hexagon.
Every pair of copies is one of these (7 = 2·3 + 1). So the graph has diameter ≤ 7 iff the freed points (p,0), the freed
lines (l,0) and the hubs have eccentricity ≤ 7 (the graph is invariant under the copy rotation, hubs fixed).

**What M must do** (exp2-squad's algebraic cover). S'(p) = {u: d(u,p)=4, d(u,l)=5} and S(l) = {N: d(N,l)=4,
d(N,p)=5} both have q⁴ = 14,641 elements; every point opposite p is on exactly one line of S(l), every line opposite l
contains exactly one point of S'(p). If every line of S(l) has M⁻¹ at distance 4 from p and every point of S'(p) has M
at distance 4 from l, then (p,0) ⇝ (u,0) → (M(u),j) → (x,j) and (l,0) ⇝ (M(y),0) → (y,−j) → (t,−j) reach everything
the hubs (which give distance ≤ 4 from p resp. l in every copy) miss. Points at distance 2 from p give "type-2" routes
(x within 3 of M(u)) as a second resource.

**Hub graph.** Any two line-side hubs and any two point-side hubs must be within 6: a bipartite graph on one hub of each
side per flag, degree ≤ 8, in which any two vertices of a side have a common neighbour. Complete bipartite (K ≤ 8),
rook grids n×m (n + m ≤ 9), cyclic difference covers for every K = 9..51 (squad c4), PG(2,7) for K = 57; K = 56 and
K ≥ 58 are impossible (c4: determinant parity; counting).

## Scaling K, and why it stops
- One flag is free (exact cover). Many flags conflict: a point lying in several S' sets needs a line at distance 4 from
  all their lines.
- Pairs of flags (`pairs.c`): covers overlap by 0 in the "chain" position (one flag's point on the other's line), by
  1,210 on both sides in generic positions (balanced), and 1,210 vs 1,331 when the two points are collinear. Triples
  overlap by ~100 ± 10 with a random imbalance (`triples.c`), presumably because H(11) is not self-dual (its points are
  regular, its lines are not). The deficits grow like the number of triples: true Hall deficiency of the cover matching
  (squad c6, exact candidates) 2,218 at K = 16, 5,973 at K = 20, 12,918 at K = 24; 8,496 points at K = 24 lie in ≥ 3
  covers and have no compatible line at all.
- Only 12 flags can have pairwise disjoint covers (12·14,641 ≤ 177,156), and at most two are pairwise in chain position.
- Placement hardly matters (c1: seeds change the leftover by 2 %; c2 greedy −13 %; r2 c3 Hall-count objective worse
  than random); clustered placements are far worse (star or points on one line: deficits 49,247 and 99,440 at K = 12,
  against 2,389 random).
- The local search (exp2-squad's d7hexr, with region/exact-matching starts, checkpoints, a targeted type-2 sweep) works
  on a proxy that counts only type-2/4 routes. The real 7-copy graph has more routes (through hubs, two cross edges,
  the hub graph), and in the tail they cover most proxy-uncovered targets: the K = 16 solution was valid in the real
  graph at proxy ≈ 35 (a watcher BFSes every checkpoint: `watch_ckpt.sh`).

## What failed and why
- Route-chart lift (bipartite Mizuno, 5 letters × F_3, fibre F_3^6; squad r1 c7): best chart 67 % nonsingular NB-6
  patterns; largest perfect Haar controller PSL(2,11) m = 660 → 962,280 vertices; m ≥ 1,702 is needed and groups of
  order ≥ 1,008 already miss elements (the controller has only 5,120 NB-6 words).
- Haar / orbit-Haar graphs: the size law (SIZE_LAW.md) puts random-like Haar graphs at about 1.5–1.6 M vertices here
  (μ ≈ 1,800 at the table order); exp2 runs at Δ = 7..11 reached 0.67–0.86 of the table.
- Per-direction freeing (r1 c8): same cost per hub (7 edges for 2 hubs), no gain. Three different cross bijections
  (r2 c6): new two-cross-edge routes cover only ~6 % of the far pairs; random ones create many more.
- Second-level hubs (vertices adjacent to hubs only) need the freed points to be 4-covering; the only small 4-covering
  sets are lines (clustered flags, bad covers); pairwise opposite points behave randomly (`cover4.c`).
- Larger copy designs are impossible (2k+1 bound), lifts of the copy graph need ≫ 1 walks per pair.
