## 0. What is known before this job

  (biadjacency ((2,1),(1,2))) over C2 × (C7 ⋊ C3) = SmallGroup(42,2), voltages 0 21 0 0 5 2 (`seeds/conder168.edges`).
  `liftx` finds exactly 168 assignments for that group and none for the other five groups of order 42.

  random 7+7, "generic" groups, orders 170–200): best cost 10. Haar wave 4: 59 groups of order 85–100 × 10 s: cost 10.
- A cubic bipartite graph has diameter ≤ 7 iff same-side distances are ≤ 6. From a vertex, at most
  1 + 6 + 24 + 96 = 127 same-side vertices lie within distance 6, hence 254.

## 3. Two structural facts

**Non-cubic graphs do not help above 169.** Let G be bipartite with Δ ≤ 3 and diameter ≤ 7, sides A, B. A vertex of
degree 2 in A has at most 1 + 4 + 16 + 64 = 85 vertices of A within distance 6, and every vertex of A must be within
distance 6 of it; a vertex of degree 1 sees at most 43. So:
- If |A| > |B|, some vertex of A has degree ≤ 2 (A carries the same number of edges as B), hence |A| ≤ 85 and
  n ≤ 169.
- If |A| = |B| and G is not cubic, both sides have deficient vertices; each has degree exactly 2, is "perfect" (its
  radius-6 ball is a tree and all vertices at distance 1–5 have degree 3), and so two deficient vertices are never
  adjacent. Adding a perfect matching between the deficient vertices of A and of B gives a cubic bipartite graph on the
  same vertex set with diameter ≤ 7.
- Hence for every even n ≥ 170: a (3,7) bipartite graph on n vertices exists iff a cubic one does. The only non-cubic
  way past 168 is n = 169 with |A| = 85, |B| = 84 and at least three perfect degree-2 vertices in A, pairwise at
  distance 6. An annealer that fixes the perfect tree of one of them (`gen169.py` + `safz.c`) ends at 530 bad pairs.

**Free extra symmetry is not a new family.** A lift over G with an extra automorphism (u, g) ↦ (σu, φ(g)), σ a
fixed-point-free colour-preserving permutation of the base of order s and φ ∈ Aut(G) with φ^s = 1, is a lift over the
semidirect product G ⋊_φ ⟨σ⟩ of the s-times smaller base B/⟨σ⟩ (left multiplication by G and the extra map generate a
semiregular group of order s|G|). So "orbit search" style lifts are covered by the exhaustive searches at the combined
order. Example: squad card r1-c5's best, C10 with a 9+9 base and a free order-3 σ (cost 18), is the exhaustive optimum of
C30 on a 3+3 base (cost 6), counted from 3 times as many sources.

  contains C3 × C3 with index 2). So n = 180 has several near misses with 72–80 bad pairs, none closer.
- Lifts with an extra symmetry that has fixed points (order 3: r2-c3; order 2: r2-c4) are far worse than the

  have size 2 (a, b conjugate), a small family; exhaustive at |G| = 58 (n = 174): diameter 10 at best.

## 6. Why the cell does not fall to these methods

1. **Every highly symmetric family is closed.** Vertex-transitive and edge-transitive graphs (censuses), all bipartite
   Cayley graphs, and every graph with a colour-preserving semiregular group of automorphisms with at most 8 orbits
   (n = 170–254) or 10 orbits (n = 170–210): exhaustive, all groups, none. In its own family the record is isolated: of the
   six groups of order 42, only C2 × (C7 ⋊ C3) gives a diameter-7 lift of the 2+2 base, and orders 43–63 give none.
   Coset graphs with stabilisers of order 2 (all groups of order 170–200 except 192, 36 M graphs) reach only diameter 8.
2. **The rest is out of reach of search.** What remains are graphs whose colour-preserving semiregular subgroups all have
   order below about n/10 (12 or more orbits). No heuristic we have can even rebuild the 168 record in that regime
   (C7, 12+12 base), and unstructured annealing stops at about 130 vertices. Low-symmetry algebraic families
   (permutation-voltage covers over AGL/PGL, polynomial graphs) behave like random graphs.
3. **The near misses are not close.** The best is 36 unordered bad pairs at n = 180 (C3 × S3), and it is a deep local
   minimum in every symmetry-broken frame we tried.
4. **Non-regular graphs give nothing above 169**, and 169 needs three "perfect" degree-2 vertices, a very rigid shape
   (section 3).

So 168 may well be optimal among graphs with a semiregular group of order ≥ n/10. Beating it would need a new algebraic
idea for graphs with small automorphism groups, or an exact search far beyond these sizes.
