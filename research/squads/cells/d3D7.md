## 3. Two structural facts

**Non-cubic graphs do not help above 169.** Let G be bipartite with Δ ≤ 3 and diameter ≤ 7, sides A, B. A vertex of
degree 2 in A has at most 1 + 4 + 16 + 64 = 85 vertices of A within distance 6, and every vertex of A must be within
distance 6 of it; a vertex of degree 1 sees at most 43. So:
- If |A| > |B|, some vertex of A has degree ≤ 2 (A carries the same number of edges as B), hence |A| ≤ 85 and
  n ≤ 169.
- If |A| = |B| and G is not cubic, both sides have deficient vertices; each has degree exactly 2, is "perfect" (its
  radius-6 ball is a tree and all vertices at distance 1–5 have degree 3), and so two deficient vertices are never
  adjacent. Adding a perfect matching between the deficient vertices of A and of B gives a cubic bipartite graph on the
  same vertex set with diameter ≤ 7.
- Hence for every even n ≥ 170: a (3,7) bipartite graph on n vertices exists iff a cubic one does. The only non-cubic
  way past 168 is n = 169 with |A| = 85, |B| = 84 and at least three perfect degree-2 vertices in A, pairwise at
  distance 6. An annealer that fixes the perfect tree of one of them (`gen169.py` + `safz.c`) ends at 530 bad pairs.

**Free extra symmetry is not a new family.** A lift over G with an extra automorphism (u, g) ↦ (σu, φ(g)), σ a
fixed-point-free colour-preserving permutation of the base of order s and φ ∈ Aut(G) with φ^s = 1, is a lift over the
semidirect product G ⋊_φ ⟨σ⟩ of the s-times smaller base B/⟨σ⟩ (left multiplication by G and the extra map generate a
semiregular group of order s|G|). So "orbit search" style lifts are covered by the exhaustive searches at the combined
order. Example: squad card r1-c5's best, C10 with a 9+9 base and a free order-3 σ (cost 18), is the exhaustive optimum of
C30 on a 3+3 base (cost 6), counted from 3 times as many sources.
