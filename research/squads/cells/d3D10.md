**Status: table beaten (in progress for larger n). A cubic bipartite graph on 810 vertices with diameter 10**
(table 800): a 5+5 voltage lift over C3 x He3 (He3 = Heisenberg group of order 27), found by joint annealing of the
base multigraph and its voltages (salift_s, D = 10). A second one over SmallGroup(81,9) = (C9 x C3):C3. Checked with
score48 and the all-pairs checker apsp.c (valid at D = 10, invalid at D = 9), and rebuilt from explicit
C3 x He3 coordinates by an independent builder. Files: `research/squads/own-small/records/` (d3D10_n810_*).

How it was found: round-1 card c3 (Haiku) ran salift, a joint base+voltage annealer from the (3,7) job, at D = 10 for
the first time and reached cost 2 at n = 810 in one 95 s run. The director re-ran it over all 15 groups of order 81
(45-120 s each): two reached cost 0, five more reached cost 2-4. Fixed-base lifts (2+2, 3+3, 4+4) and every symmetric
family had stalled at cost >= 2 around n = 812-880; letting the base change together with the voltages is what worked.

## 0. Counting: where the cell sits

A cubic bipartite graph has diameter <= 10 iff every vertex reaches the whole other side by odd walks of length <= 9
and its own side by even walks of length <= 10. From one vertex there are at most 1,023 vertices of the other side
within distance 9 and 2,047 of its own side within distance 10, so the binding constraint is the other side: n/2 <= 1,023.
At n = 800 the graph needs 39% of that maximum; the n = 880 near miss reaches 43% with one miss per vertex. For
comparison the records at D = 7, 8 (ours), 9 sit at 66%, 56%, 49.5% of their binding count. So 800 is roughly on the
trend of the sequence, not an obvious outlier.
