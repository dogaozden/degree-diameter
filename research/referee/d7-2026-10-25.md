## Issues

Each issue gives the location (section, and the first words of the passage), the problem, and a suggested fix.

### 1. ERROR (factual, prior art; not mathematical). §11, paragraph "*New here (as far as we know).*"

The paragraph lists as new: "Deleting matching edges at freed elements and reattaching the freed slots to hub vertices
(Definition 3.1, Theorem 3.4); the repair theorems B and C; the use of dual levels ('typed levels') to make transitions
automorphic". The lab's literature check (research/lit/novelty-2026-10-25.md, claims 1 and 2a) found:

- R. Rajiv, "New lower bounds for the degree-diameter problem from generalized quadrangles and hexagons", Zenodo,
  30 September 2026, https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23052995. Its Definition 3 is a compound of $m$ copies of a generalized
  polygon's incidence graph along a tournament, with type-changing matchings (point to line) and type-preserving
  matchings (point to point, line to line). Its Lemma 5 deletes the type-preserving matching edges at a pairwise
  non-collinear (or non-concurrent) set and keeps the diameter, with a shortest-path proof. Its Theorem 3 attaches new
  vertices, each joined to one point and one line of every block, connected by a graph $Y$. In this note's language these
  are: friendly transitions with $M_t = \mathrm{id}$ (the mechanism of typed levels), Corollary 4.3(1) (the non-collinear
  case of Theorem C at friendly transitions), and hubs as in Definition 3.1 and Theorem 3.4. The setting differs
  (general graphs of diameter 6, not bipartite graphs of diameter 7; hubs with a point slot and a line slot, not one-sided
  $\mathcal A$/$\mathcal B$ hubs), but the ideas are the same, and the preprint is about three weeks older than the
  lab's diameter-7 work.
- F. Comellas' table of general graphs, entry for $(4,6)$ of 2 October 2026 (incidence edges of $H(3)$ deleted "to free
  degree", the capacity used to attach new vertices), and the 7–15 October entries by Bhakar and Yugeswardeenoo, also
  delete or subdivide hexagon edges and attach new vertices.

The literature report's own wording rule is "Do not say the hub or friendly-surgery idea is unpublished."

*Fix.* Add the references, and rewrite the paragraph along these lines: "The closest prior work is [Ra26], which
deletes type-preserving matching edges at non-collinear sets and attaches hub vertices, for general graphs of diameter 6
(see also the 2026 entries of Comellas' table). In the sources we searched (research/lit/novelty-2026-10-25.md) we found
nothing on: the bipartite diameter-7 version with one-sided hubs; Theorem B and mirrored sets (local dualities at
non-automorphic transitions, Propositions 5.1–5.2); segment sets, Proposition 1.4, Theorem C beyond non-collinear sets and
its converse (Remark 4.2); and the nine values of Section 0." Keep "nothing found in the sources we searched" rather than
"new". (The phrase "the lab's new lower bounds" in the first paragraph refers to the values and can stay.)

### 2. GAP (prior art). §11 "*Classical.*" and the paragraph after it; §2, sentence "When all levels have type $H$ …"

(a) §11 says "the survey's table does not name the source of each cell, and we have not determined it." The lab has
determined it: the CombinatoricsWiki table (same numbers as [MS13, Table 4]) credits the D = 7 cells with Δ = 6–16 to
C. Delorme, J. Gómez, J.-J. Quisquater, "On large bipartite graphs", marked "submitted" (no published version found).

(b) The general-graph form of Definition 2.1 (one matching per arc of a tournament between copies of a graph) is the
compound "B(T)" that Comellas' table credits to J. Gómez, M. A. Fiol, "Dense compound graphs", Ars Combin. 20-A (1985)
211–237. It should be cited next to the star product.

(c) According to the literature check, which read the full text of [De85a], that paper contains three things the note
uses without credit: §3, multi-level graphs $\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\times Q^k$ built
with a duality of a generalized quadrangle or hexagon (from that description, the all-$H$ duality level graph of Corollary 8.2 appears to be of this kind; check against the
text);
§8(b), duplication of $q^3+1$ pairwise opposite vertices of the split Cayley hexagon, citing [Th80] (the earliest use of
opposite sets in this problem that the literature check found); §9, vertices inserted on edges of the incidence graph of
$H(q)$, which gives the table's (6,7) = 8,310 and (8,7) = 40,586 (a diameter-7 "hexagon plus new vertices" construction).

*Fix.* In §11 replace "the survey's table does not name the source … we have not determined it" by "the wiki's colour key
credits these cells to Delorme, Gómez and Quisquater [DGQ, submitted]"; cite [GF85] for compounds along a tournament;
cite [De85a, §3] at Corollary 8.2, [De85a, §8(b)] at Corollary 5.3(a) and Lemma 1.2, and [De85a, §9] in §11. In §2
replace "this is the graph whose order is the table value" by "this graph has the order of the table value; the table
credits these cells to [DGQ]".

### 3. GAP (small). Corollary 9.1, and its use of Corollary 8.1

Corollary 8.1 is stated with exactly $\lvert R\rvert$ hubs of each colour ("If the hub graph $Y$ (with $\lvert R\rvert$
hubs of each colour) …"). Corollary 9.1 uses $\min(q^2+1, \kappa(q-1))$ hubs, which is smaller than $\lvert R\rvert$ at
$q = 4$ ($\kappa(3) = 7 < 17$). The step is easy but missing: either apply Corollary 8.1 to a 7-point subset of $R$ (still a
partial ovoid, and $\sigma$ still satisfies (H1)–(H3) on it), or restate Corollary 8.1 with $K\le\lvert R\rvert$ hubs of
each colour, which Definition 3.1 allows. Also say that at $q = 4$ the bound, $6N(4) + 14 = 8{,}204$, is below the table's
(6,7) = 8,310, or start the corollary at $q = 5$.

### 5. MINOR. §9, paragraph "The record values exceed these because the sets found by computer are of order $q^4$ …"

### 6. MINOR. §0, bullet "Theorem B": "Propositions 5.1 and 5.2 say when such an $M$ exists ('mirrored sets')"

characterize existence. *Fix:* "Proposition 5.1 gives a necessary condition for such an $M$, and Proposition 5.2 a

### 7. MINOR. Corollary 8.1, last sentence: "… the bijection $f$ onto Hermitian spread lines, the hub graph"

Only $f_0$ maps onto Hermitian spread lines. The lines $L_x$ are not spread lines (research/cell147/mirror/README.md:
"lines $L_x$ … pairwise opposite, not in the Hermitian spread"), and they cannot be: if they were, all of $R^*$ would be
pairwise opposite and (S2) would force $R$ to be a partial ovoid. *Fix:* "the bijection $f_0$ onto Hermitian spread
lines, the lines $L_x$ (pairwise opposite, not in the spread), the hub graph".

### 11. MINOR. §1.3, "Ovoids of $Q(6,q)$ (equivalently of $H(q)$) exist for $q = 3^h$; they do not exist for $q$ even [Th81] or for $q$ prime $\ge 5$ [BGS06]."

The existence statement has no reference. Lemma 1.2(d) gives the existence (a duality maps the Hermitian spread to an
ovoid), so cite Lemma 1.2(d) with [Th81] there. Also, (b) of Lemma 1.2 is cited as "[Th80]"; give the result's number in
[Th80] if possible. After Proposition 1.4, where the note says "equality means a distance-2 ovoid", it is worth adding
that distance-2 ovoids of $H(q)$ are known only for $q = 2, 3, 4$ (A. De Wispelaere, H. Van Maldeghem, Discrete Math. 308
(2008) 2976–2983, as cited by Bishnoi–Ihringer, arXiv:1606.07288), so the bound $q^4+q^2+1$ is not expected to be
reached in general.
