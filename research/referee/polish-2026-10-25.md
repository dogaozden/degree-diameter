### Prior art and credit (fix before sending)

**1. MINOR (credit). Delorme, not [ADFL21], is the earliest use of line copies in a GQ.** L.421–423 (§3 opening),
Change 11 (L.59–60), and the note on [ADFL21] (L.1228–1229); `geometry.tex` §5.2 has the same sentence.
The note says copies of lines in a GQ "appear already in [ADFL21]", and Change 11 calls [ADFL21] "the closest prior
use of line copies". Delorme's $D=4$ table construction did this in 1985, and the note itself presents it as Example
3.4: it doubles a spread of $W(q)$, i.e. it adds a copy of each of $q^2+1$ lines of a GQ (and of the points of $v$).
[D85a, §7] allows any induced subgraph to be doubled, points and lines together. The novelty report (Claim 4b) gives
the verdict "partial overlap: Delorme 1985 has point/line duplication in GQ incidence graphs". A level-0 line whose
points are all at level $-1$ is exactly a shadow line with $\sigma=\mathrm{id}$.
*Fix:* "Copies of points and lines of a GQ go back to Delorme [D85a, §8(a)], whose $D=4$ construction (Example 3.4)
doubles a spread; Araujo-Pardo, Dalfó, Fiol and López [ADFL21, Sec. 4] double every line to get biregular graphs.
Theorem B is not a doubling: a clone is joined to every old line through its point, so no tree condition on the
copied set is needed, and it allows weights larger than one, a multiset of lines, and stand-ins." Delete "the closest
prior use of line copies" from Change 11. Make the same change in `geometry.tex`.

### Smaller points about statements and proofs

**7. MINOR. §2.2, L.298–302 (regular completion in an arbitrary plane).** "$2(q-4)\le(q-1)(q-3)$, so Hall's theorem
gives a perfect matching" compresses the Hall argument that `geometry.tex` Remark 4.8 writes out. A shorter correct
argument: the non-incidence graph between deficient points and deficient lines is regular of degree
$N-(q-4)\ge1$ ($N=(q-1)(q-3)$), and a regular bipartite graph of positive degree has a perfect matching (König).
Either way, name the criterion. Also, at $q=3$ the coordinate-free structure has no deficient element at all (the four
$X_s$ are all of $\ell$), so it is already $(q+2)$-regular; `hat4free.py` confirms this in $\mathrm{PG}(2,3)$ (36
vertices, 5-regular, diameter 3). So "for every $q\ge4$" can be "for every $q\ge3$". This affects no cell.
