### Must fix before sending

**1. GAP (prior art): §6.1 "Matrix groups" (lines 738–741) and §6.2 item 1 (lines 755–759).**
The note cites only Bourgain–Gamburd and Helfgott for matrix groups. The lab's own novelty check (claim 5, verdict
"partial overlap") found that the same construction, a dipole lift (Haar graph) over SL(2,q) or PSL(2,q) × Z_k, is
already in the literature, for girth:
- Exoo–Jajcay, *On the girth of voltage graph lifts*, European J. Combin. 32 (2011) 554–562. The smallest known
  cubic graph of girth 30 is a lift of the θ-graph (two vertices, three parallel edges) over SL(2,83), so it is a
  Haar graph H(SL(2,83), S) with |S| = 3.
- Bray–Parker–Rowley, Discrete Math. 214 (2000) 113–121. A cubic girth-27 "Cayley type" graph built from
  PSL(2,83) × Z_3.

The novelty check also lists related uses of these groups:
- Lubotzky–Phillips–Sarnak (1988): bipartite Cayley graphs of PGL(2,q).
- Erskine–Tuite, arXiv:1708.02425: matrix groups for degree–diameter, diameters 3–5.
- The 2026 Comellas-table entries: a voltage lift over SL(2,7):C₂ at general (4,7), and SL(2,q)-invariant hexagon
  constructions at D = 6.

A finite geometer reading §6 will know the cage literature, and its absence will look like an over-claim. The
sentence "We have not found earlier use of SL(2,q)-type groups for bipartite records at D ≥ 8" is literally true,
but it is incomplete.

*Fix:*
- Add a paragraph citing these works, with the novelty check's evidence tags ([meta]/[read] as there). Cite
  `research/lit/novelty-2026-10-25.md` among the literature notes (line 711).
- Reword §6.2(1) along these lines: "Haar graphs (dipole lifts) of SL(2,q) and PSL(2,q) × Z_n were used before for
  small cubic graphs of large girth [Bray–Parker–Rowley; Exoo–Jajcay]; we found no earlier use of them for the
  bipartite degree–diameter problem (sources searched: …)."

- Split c = c⁺ − c⁻ with Σc⁺ = j + ε and Σc⁻ = j, where 0 ≤ j ≤ K = ⌊(D − 1 − ε)/2⌋.
