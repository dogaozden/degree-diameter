  extraspecial group 3^{1+2} of exponent 3.
- λ = 1 + i has order 8 in F_9^* and norm λ⁴ = −1, so φ(v, z) = (λv, −z) is an automorphism of order 8.

- S = {(0,0,0), (2i,0,9), (2+i,0,8), (1+2i,2,14)}. The graph is H(G, S): (g, A) ~ (s·g, B).

- In both groups r generates the nonzero squares, and p ≡ 3 (mod 4). So ±⟨r⟩ = F_p^*: one step of the walk
  already reaches every nonzero translation.

### 2.2 Three general facts used throughout

- **Normal form.** Take G = F_q ⋊ C_k with C_k acting by multiplication by r (r ≠ 1, any q, faithful or not).
  Every connected H(G, S) is isomorphic to one with S = {(0,0), (0, j1), (1, j2), s3, …}, where r^{j1} ≠ 1.
  *Proof:*
  - H(G, S) ≅ H(G, xSy) ≅ H(G, σS) for x, y ∈ G and σ ∈ Aut G. So assume (0,0) ∈ S.
  - Some element has j-part acting non-trivially, otherwise S lies in the abelian subgroup F_q × ker and the
    graph is disconnected. Conjugating by the translation (c, 0) sends (a, j) to (a + c(1 − r^j), j), which
    kills its a-part.
  - Some other element has a ≠ 0 (else S ⊂ C_k, disconnected). Scaling a ↦ ua is an automorphism; use it to
    make that a-part 1. ∎
  The search space is therefore (k−1)·k·N^{Δ−3} sets. For Δ = 3 that is only k².
- **Cover structure.** H(F_q ⋊ C_k, S) is an F_q-voltage lift of the bipartite circulant H(Z_k, S̄), where
  S̄ is the multiset of j-parts. The edge from (j, A) labelled s = (a_s, j_s) carries the voltage
  a_s·r^{−(j + j_s)}. (Right multiplication by F_q is free with quotient H(Z_k, S̄); take fibre representatives
  (0, j).) So these Haar graphs are exactly the cyclic-group lifts of circulants whose voltages turn by r when the
  base rotates.
- **Quotient bound** (`hx.c` QMAP filter, `abfilt.c`). Non-backtracking walks of H(G, S) project to H(G/K, S̄)
  for every normal subgroup K. Each target vertex within distance D−1 is the end of its geodesic, so every
  K-coset on the target side needs at least |K| such walks.
  - With K = G′ this is a fast test per group and per image S̄.
  - With G abelian it gives the **generic lattice bound**: |G| is at most the number of walk endpoints in
    Z^{Δ−1}, which is 61 for (3,9) and 147 for (4,7). So abelian groups are out at once.
  - The filter never removed a known solution: all 132 and all 48 above are kept.

### 2.3 Complete negatives at (3,7)

- **Haar.** Every group of order 85–127 was searched (GAP SmallGroups; complete, no group skipped or timed out:
  `runs/allgroups_d3D7.tsv`). 259 groups fail the rank test, 136 the abelianization bound, and the other 117
  have no diameter-7 set; the best is cost 100. Since 2N ≤ 254 (Moore), **no cubic Haar graph with more than
  168 vertices has diameter 7.** The largest cubic Haar graph of diameter 7 in the F_q ⋊ C_k family has 140
  vertices (Z5 ⋊ Z14).
- **2+2 lifts.** Every group of order 43–63 was searched with the 2+2 cubic base (biadjacency ((2,1),(1,2))),

  order 48. Order 42 gives exactly one group, C2 × (C7 : C3), which is Conder's 168. **So Conder's graph is
  the largest 2+2 cubic lift of diameter 7.**

## 3. Conjectured, with strong evidence

### 3.1 Records come from affine-type groups over tiny bases

| cell | record | base | group |
|---|---|---|---|
| (3,7) | 168 (Conder) | 2+2 | (F_7 ⋊ C_3) × C_2 (rebuilt: `lx`, 168 assignments) |
| (3,8) | 288 (ours) | 2+2 | F_9 ⋊ C_8 (action of order 4) |
| (3,9) | 506 (Loz) | dipole | AGL(1,23) ⊃ F_23 ⋊ C_11 |
| (4,7) | **864 (new)** | dipole | Heis(F_9) ⋊ C_16, and two others of order 432 |
| (4,9) | 4970 (Loz) | dipole | F_71 ⋊ C_35 |
| (4,7) | 840 (Bond–Delorme) | dipole | A5 × Z7 (the exception) |

In each affine case the group has a large abelian, or class-2, normal subgroup on which a cyclic group acts by
field multiplication. The walk sums are then signed sums of powers of a field element. They spread over the
fibres much better than random words: at (3,9) random S would leave about 34 uncovered (μ = N e^{−W/N}), yet
5 % of all normal-form sets work.

## 4. The voltage-lifts metacyclic records (45 solutions, D ≥ 7)

- **Normal forms.** `meta.py` re-checks all 47 saved metacyclic Haar solutions with its own BFS (all VALID,
  `runs/meta_parse_check.txt`).
- **Fibre balance** (`fibers.py` → `runs/fibers_out.txt`). For the quotient Z_k and each quotient
  Z_d ⋊ Z_k with d | m prime, it compares the walk counts per fibre with random S in the same group.
  - The records are a little better balanced than random S: the percentile of their μ_d among random S is
    0.0–0.6, and their μ_d/μ_unif is at most 1.13 at d = 1.
  - For large k many random S already fail the rigorous bound. At Z97 ⋊ Z64, 42 % do.
- **Does balance predict success?** (`predict.py` → `runs/predict_out.txt`, 282 anneal runs of the
  voltage-lifts sweeps). No.
  - Within each cell, the solved and stalled runs have the same median share of S failing the bound
    (0.13–0.34 against 0.11–0.38) and the same median excess.
  - The difference between them is in N, through μ (for example 25.7 against 30.7 at (5,10)).
  - So "which (m, k, r) solve" is decided by N and the seed, not by a group invariant we could find. This
    agrees with REGULARITIES.md and SIZE_LAW.md.

## 5. Weak patterns

- The Δ = 3 normal-form j-patterns are sometimes arithmetic progressions with the translation on the middle
  element ((3,9): {0, 1, 6} in Z11 has 6 as the midpoint of 0 and 1). This is not a rule: Z43 ⋊ Z7 and
  F_27 ⋊ C13 at D = 10 have no AP solution (`jpat.py`).
- Cay(AGL(1,p), {x ↦ gx, x ↦ g⁻¹x, x ↦ −x+1}) over p ≡ 3 (mod 4) gives diameters 6, 7, 9, 9, 11 for
  p = 7, 11, 19, 23, 31 (`runs/agl_cayley.txt`). Only p = 23 is a record. This is not a family that covers a row.
