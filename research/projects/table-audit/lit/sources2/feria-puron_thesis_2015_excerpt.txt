3.2.3    Large Bipartite Graphs

The table of bipartite graphs is distinguished by the existence of Moore bipartite graphs for di-
ameters 3, 4 and 6 for most degrees. Apart from this, the situation is not much more favourable
than for general graphs. Historical overviews for construction of large bipartite graphs can be
found in the papers [17, 94, 143], and more recently in [54, 115]. Many constructions of large
bipartite graphs were proposed by Delorme by using graph compounding and other techniques
[35, 36]. Later, Delorme and Bond [18, 19] provided several constructions exploiting the con-
cept of partial Cayley graphs. Other constructions for particular degrees or diameters have
also yielded a record bipartite graph, as in the case of [4, 53, 54].
Computer search was present in [21, 54, 107, 108], but as in the case of general graphs, the
majority of the non-Moore record graphs in the table are the fruit of the work of Loz and
Širáň in [103] described in Section 3.1.3. Clearly, the lift of a bipartite base graph containing
no loops is also bipartite. Loz and Širáň also considered other necessary conditions for a lift
graph of a non-bipartite graph to be bipartite.
As mentioned in Section 3.1.3, Loz and Širáň only considered groups with a small or trivial
center. In Chapter 4 we present a bipartite graph of degree 11, diameter 3 and order 190,
which improves the former lower bound for N(11, 3, B) by four vertices. Such graph can be
obtained alternatively as a lift of a dipole by an abelian group. Somehow surprisingly, Loz
and Širáň were not able to beat the previous value 186 for this entry in the table of largest
known bipartite graphs.

Problem 4.2
Given a natural number ∆ ≥ 6, find the largest natural number m(∆) for which there exist
natural numbers a1 , a2 , . . . , a∆−3 (2 ≤ aj ≤ m−2) such that the graph Φm(∆) (a1 , a2 , . . . , a∆−3 )
has diameter 3.

If we take a vertex x0 of a Φm(∆) (a1 , a2 , . . . , a∆−3 ) and assume that x0 has neighbors y0 ,
y1 , y−1 , ya1 , ya2 ,. . ., ya∆−3 then x0 can reach in exactly two steps the – not necessarily
distinct – vertices x0 , x1 , x−1 , x2 , x−2 , xai , x−ai , xai +1 , x−ai −1 , xai −1 , x−ai +1 and xai −aj , and
no other vertex. Problem 4.2 amounts to the following congruence-related problem since
Φm(∆) (a1 , a2 , . . . , a∆−3 ) is vertex-transitive.
Problem 4.3
Given a natural number ∆ ≥ 6, find the largest natural number m(∆) for which there exist

4.9      Catalogues and Upper Bounds
The results in this chapter complete the catalogue of bipartite (∆, D, −4)-graphs with ∆ ≥ 2
and 5 ≤ D ≤ 187, which in turn completes the catalogue of bipartite (∆, D, −)-graphs with
∆ ≥ 2, 5 ≤ D ≤ 187, D 6= 6 and 0 ≤  ≤ 4.

Catalogue of bipartite (∆, D, 0)-graphs with ∆ ≥ 2 and 5 ≤ D ≤ 187. For 5 ≤ D ≤ 187
    and ∆ = 2 the only Moore bipartite graphs are the 2D-cycles, whereas for D = 6 and
    ∆ ≥ 3 they are incidence graphs of generalized polygons. The existence of such polygons
    has been shown only when ∆ − 1 is a prime power, which makes this catalogue to remain
    incomplete. For other values of 5 ≤ D ≤ 187 and ∆ ≥ 3 there are no Moore bipartite
    graphs.
Catalogue of bipartite (∆, D, −2)-graphs with ∆ ≥ 2 and 5 ≤ D ≤ 187. The results of
    [126] combined with [40, 41] showed that there are no such graphs.

Catalogue of bipartite (∆, D, −4)-graphs with ∆ ≥ 2 and 5 ≤ D ≤ 187. The only such
    graphs is the path of length 5.

Another important result in this chapter is the completion of the catalogue of bipartite
(3, D, −)-graphs with D ≥ 2 and 0 ≤  ≤ 4.

Catalogue of bipartite (3, D, 0)-graphs with D ≥ 2. The cubic Moore bipartite graphs
    are the Thomsen graph for D = 2, the unique incidence graph of the projective plane
    of order 2 for D = 3, the unique incidence graph of the generalized quadrangle of order
    2 for D = 4, and the unique incidence graph of the generalized hexagon of order 2 for
    D = 6.

Catalogue of bipartite (3, D, −2)-graphs with D ≥ 2. There are only two non-isomorphic
    (3, D, −2)-graphs with D ≥ 2; a unique bipartite (3, 2, −2)-graph (the claw graph), and
    a unique (3, 3, −2)-graph, which is depicted in Figure 3.3 (a).

Catalogue of bipartite (3, D, −4)-graphs with D ≥ 2. There exist no bipartite (3, 2, −4)-
    graphs. When the diameter is 3, there are four non-isomorphic bipartite (3, 3, −4)-
    graphs; all of them are shown in Figure 3.4. For diameter 4, there is a unique bi-
    partite (3, 4, −4)-graph, which is depicted in Figure 3.5. The results presented here,
    combined with those of [87], assert that there are no bipartite (3, D, −4)-graphs with
    D ≥ 5, outcome that gives an alternative proof of the optimality of the known bipartite
    (3, 5, −6)-graph (see [18]).
